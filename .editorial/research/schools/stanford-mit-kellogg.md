# Stanford GSB, MIT Sloan & Northwestern Kellogg — Verified Bibliography

Curated bibliography of influential and frontier marketing scholarship associated with **Stanford Graduate School of Business**, the **MIT Sloan School of Management**, and the **Northwestern Kellogg School of Management**, mapped to book chapters. The set spans the three schools' complementary traditions: Stanford's behavioral-decision and structural-advertising research, MIT Sloan's field-experiment / networks / trust program, and Kellogg's consumer-behavior, pricing/retail, and statistical-methods scholarship.

**Verification protocol.** Every entry below was matched against the Crossref REST API (`https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=...`) and each DOI was independently resolved via `https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`. Title, author list, year, container-title (journal), volume, issue, and page range were checked against the returned version-of-record metadata. Published versions-of-record are used in preference to any SSRN / NBER / preprint DOI; where Crossref returned only a working-paper DOI, the published DOI was located by a follow-up query and substituted. Items that could not be verified to a published DOI are quarantined at the bottom under **UNVERIFIED — do not cite**.

Scholars and works already gathered elsewhere in the research set (John Hauser and Catherine Tucker; Alexander Chernev's `chernev2015`; Tellis; Marinova; the conjoint set; the branding-frontier and advertising sets) are not re-listed here. Where a candidate work was a Hauser-led paper (e.g., website morphing), it was deliberately omitted to avoid duplication, and a non-overlapping work by the same school was substituted.

## Stanford GSB — Behavioral decision research (consumer behavior, neuro)

```bibtex
@article{simonson1989reasons,
  author  = {Simonson, Itamar},
  title   = {Choice Based on Reasons: The Case of Attraction and Compromise Effects},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {1989},
  volume  = {16},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {158--174},
  doi     = {10.1086/209205}
}
```
The foundational demonstration that introducing a third, asymmetrically dominated or intermediate option systematically shifts choice shares among the existing options — the attraction and compromise effects. Simonson argues consumers select options they can justify with reasons, which makes context-dependent rather than context-free preferences the norm. A cornerstone of behavioral decision theory in marketing and a direct challenge to value-maximization models of choice.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{simonson1992context,
  author  = {Simonson, Itamar and Tversky, Amos},
  title   = {Choice in Context: Tradeoff Contrast and Extremeness Aversion},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1992},
  volume  = {29},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {281--295},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224379202900301}
}
```
Generalizes the context effects into two principles: tradeoff contrast (the attractiveness of a tradeoff depends on the other tradeoffs in the set) and extremeness aversion (middle options gain share because extremes are easier to reject). With Tversky, Simonson grounds compromise and polarization effects in a unified account of how the offered set reshapes preference. Among the most-cited results connecting psychology and marketing strategy on assortment and line design.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{shiv1999heart,
  author  = {Shiv, Baba and Fedorikhin, Alexander},
  title   = {Heart and Mind in Conflict: The Interplay of Affect and Cognition in Consumer Decision Making},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {1999},
  volume  = {26},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {278--292},
  doi     = {10.1086/209563}
}
```
Shows that when processing resources are constrained, affect-laden options (chocolate cake vs. fruit salad) win out, but when cognitive capacity is available, deliberative considerations reassert control. The study operationalizes the dual-system view of decision making in a consumer setting and ties impulsive choice to limited cognitive resources. A seminal bridge between affect, cognition, and the self-control literature in consumer behavior.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{karmarkar2015cost,
  author  = {Karmarkar, Uma R. and Shiv, Baba and Knutson, Brian},
  title   = {Cost Conscious? The Neural and Behavioral Impact of Price Primacy on Decision Making},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {52},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {467--481},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.13.0488}
}
```
Uses fMRI and behavioral experiments to show that whether price or product information is presented first changes the neural computation underlying purchase: price-first framing shifts evaluation toward a cost–benefit "is it worth it?" calculation, while product-first framing emphasizes desirability. A frontier neuromarketing contribution from the Stanford GSB neuroscience program connecting sequencing of information to brain activity and choice.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Stanford GSB — Brand, time, and happiness (Jennifer Aaker)

```bibtex
@article{aaker1997brandpersonality,
  author  = {Aaker, Jennifer L.},
  title   = {Dimensions of Brand Personality},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1997},
  volume  = {34},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {347--356},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224379703400304}
}
```
Develops and validates a five-factor framework for brand personality — sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication, and ruggedness — and a measurement scale to assess how consumers perceive brands as having human-like traits. The most influential operationalization of brand personality and a workhorse construct in branding research and practice.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{mogilner2009timemoney,
  author  = {Mogilner, Cassie and Aaker, Jennifer},
  title   = {The Time vs. Money Effect: Shifting Product Attitudes and Decisions through Personal Connection},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {277--291},
  doi     = {10.1086/597161}
}
```
Demonstrates that priming time (rather than money) typically strengthens product attitudes and purchase intentions because time evokes a more personal, experiential connection to a product. The "time vs. money effect" reframes how marketers should position offerings and is a foundational result in the consumer time-and-happiness research stream Aaker helped establish.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{aaker2012admiration,
  author  = {Aaker, Jennifer L. and Garbinsky, Emily N. and Vohs, Kathleen D.},
  title   = {Cultivating Admiration in Brands: Warmth, Competence, and Landing in the ``Golden Quadrant''},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Psychology},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {22},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {191--194},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.jcps.2011.11.012}
}
```
Imports the warmth–competence framework from social cognition into branding, showing that brands perceived as high on both dimensions — the "golden quadrant" — earn consumer admiration and the behavioral loyalty that follows. Connects brand personality work to a parsimonious two-dimensional account of how consumers judge brands as social agents.
Chapter: branding

## Stanford GSB — Advertising, structural & digital (Hartmann, Sahni, Nair, Gardete)

```bibtex
@article{hartmann2018superbowl,
  author  = {Hartmann, Wesley R. and Klapper, Daniel},
  title   = {Super Bowl Ads},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {37},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {78--96},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2017.1055}
}
```
Exploits variation in local Super Bowl viewership (driven by which teams reach the game) as a natural experiment to identify the sales impact of Super Bowl advertising in the beer and soda categories. Hartmann and Klapper find substantial returns and argue the ads build a complementarity between the brand and broader sports viewership. A model of credible identification of advertising effects from observational data.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{sahni2016spillovers,
  author  = {Sahni, Navdeep S.},
  title   = {Advertising Spillovers: Evidence from Online Field Experiments and Implications for Returns on Advertising},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {53},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {459--478},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.14.0274}
}
```
Uses large-scale online field experiments to show that advertising for one product raises demand for related, non-advertised products of the same firm — spillover effects that conventional ROI calculations ignore. Sahni argues that omitting spillovers materially understates the true returns to advertising. A frontier digital-advertising measurement study with direct managerial implications for budgeting.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{sahni2019retargeting,
  author  = {Sahni, Navdeep S. and Narayanan, Sridhar and Kalyanam, Kirthi},
  title   = {An Experimental Investigation of the Effects of Retargeted Advertising: The Role of Frequency and Timing},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2019},
  volume  = {56},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {401--418},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022243718813987}
}
```
A field experiment dissecting retargeted (behaviorally targeted follow-up) display advertising, isolating how frequency and timing of exposures shape conversions. The study provides causal evidence on when retargeting helps versus wears out, informing the design of digital remarketing campaigns. A frontier contribution on the mechanics of online ad delivery.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{nair2007intertemporal,
  author  = {Nair, Harikesh},
  title   = {Intertemporal Price Discrimination with Forward-Looking Consumers: Application to the US Market for Console Video-Games},
  journal = {Quantitative Marketing and Economics},
  year    = {2007},
  volume  = {5},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {239--292},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11129-007-9026-4}
}
```
Estimates a dynamic structural model in which forward-looking consumers strategically delay purchases of console video games in anticipation of price declines, and the firm sets a declining price path in response. Nair quantifies the value of intertemporal price discrimination for durable-goods markets with high-valuation early adopters. A canonical application of dynamic-demand estimation in marketing.
Chapter: structural-models

```bibtex
@article{gardete2013cheaptalk,
  author  = {Gardete, Pedro M.},
  title   = {Cheap-Talk Advertising and Misrepresentation in Vertically Differentiated Markets},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2013},
  volume  = {32},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {609--621},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2013.0772}
}
```
An analytical model of advertising as cheap talk, asking when unverifiable claims about product quality can still credibly transmit information in markets with vertically differentiated products. Gardete characterizes the conditions under which informative equilibria survive and when firms misrepresent. A theory contribution to the economics of advertising and information disclosure.
Chapter: analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{gardete2015inflight,
  author  = {Gardete, Pedro M.},
  title   = {Social Effects in the In-Flight Marketplace: Characterization and Managerial Implications},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {52},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {360--374},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.13.0527}
}
```
Uses transaction data from an in-flight retail setting to identify peer/social effects on purchasing — passengers are influenced by the purchases of nearby travelers — and works out the pricing and assortment implications. A clean empirical characterization of local social influence in a confined marketplace.
Chapter: empirical-models

```bibtex
@article{gardete2018tailored,
  author  = {Gardete, Pedro M. and Bart, Yakov},
  title   = {Tailored Cheap Talk: The Effects of Privacy Policy on Ad Content and Market Outcomes},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {37},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {733--752},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2018.1102}
}
```
Models how privacy policy — specifically how much consumer information advertisers may use — shapes the persuasiveness and content of tailored advertising and the resulting market outcomes. Gardete and Bart show that more permissive data use is not unambiguously good for firms or consumers, with credibility of tailored messages at stake. A frontier analytical link between privacy regulation and advertising.
Chapter: privacy

## MIT Sloan — Field experiments, pricing, and targeting (Simester)

```bibtex
@article{anderson2003priceendings,
  author  = {Anderson, Eric T. and Simester, Duncan I.},
  title   = {Effects of \$9 Price Endings on Retail Sales: Evidence from Field Experiments},
  journal = {Quantitative Marketing and Economics},
  year    = {2003},
  volume  = {1},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {93--110},
  doi     = {10.1023/A:1023581927405}
}
```
Reports controlled catalog field experiments showing that prices ending in 9 raise demand — sometimes a price of \$39 sells more than \$34 — evidence that price endings carry an "on sale" cue beyond their numeric value. A landmark demonstration that behavioral pricing effects are real and economically meaningful in the field, not just the lab.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{anderson2004promotiondepth,
  author  = {Anderson, Eric T. and Simester, Duncan I.},
  title   = {Long-Run Effects of Promotion Depth on New Versus Established Customers: Three Field Studies},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {23},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {4--20},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1030.0040}
}
```
Three field studies find that deep price promotions can have negative long-run effects on established customers (who feel they overpaid before) even as they attract new ones — the long-run consequence depends on customer tenure. A foundational result on the dynamic and segment-specific costs of promotions.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{simester2020targeting,
  author  = {Simester, Duncan and Timoshenko, Artem and Zoumpoulis, Spyros I.},
  title   = {Targeting Prospective Customers: Robustness of Machine-Learning Methods to Typical Data Challenges},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2020},
  volume  = {66},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {2495--2522},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2019.3308}
}
```
Evaluates how well machine-learning targeting methods hold up against the data problems firms actually face — small training samples, many features, distribution shift — using two large field experiments as ground truth. Simester and co-authors give practical guidance on which methods are robust for customer targeting. A frontier link between modern ML and field-validated marketing decisions.
Chapter: data

## MIT Sloan — Online trust, networks, and social learning (Urban, Eckles, Zhang)

```bibtex
@article{urban2009onlinetrust,
  author  = {Urban, Glen L. and Amyx, Cinda and Lorenzon, Antonio},
  title   = {Online Trust: State of the Art, New Frontiers, and Research Potential},
  journal = {Journal of Interactive Marketing},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {23},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {179--190},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.intmar.2009.03.001}
}
```
A synthesizing statement of Urban's long program on building consumer trust online, arguing that trust extends beyond privacy and security to website design and is an ongoing, cultivable relationship. Lays out a research agenda for trust-based marketing in digital environments. A foundational reference for the online-trust literature.
Chapter: marketing-strategy

```bibtex
@article{eckles2016peereffects,
  author  = {Eckles, Dean and Kizilcec, Ren{\'e} F. and Bakshy, Eytan},
  title   = {Estimating Peer Effects in Networks with Peer Encouragement Designs},
  journal = {Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {113},
  number  = {27},
  pages   = {7316--7322},
  doi     = {10.1073/pnas.1511201113}
}
```
Introduces "peer encouragement designs" — randomized interventions that nudge some users' behavior to identify the downstream causal effect on their network neighbors — to overcome the confounding that plagues observational estimates of social influence. Eckles and co-authors demonstrate the method at scale on a social platform. A frontier causal-inference contribution for measuring contagion and word-of-mouth.
Chapter: virality

```bibtex
@article{zhang2010silence,
  author  = {Zhang, Juanjuan},
  title   = {The Sound of Silence: Observational Learning in the U.S. Kidney Market},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2010},
  volume  = {29},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {315--335},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1090.0500}
}
```
Shows that prospective kidney recipients infer quality from the silent fact that earlier patients in the queue declined an organ, leading them to decline too — observational learning producing inefficient herding. Zhang structurally separates this informational mechanism from private-value explanations. A landmark empirical demonstration of social learning with high-stakes managerial and policy implications.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{zhang2011personalization,
  author  = {Zhang, Juanjuan},
  title   = {The Perils of Behavior-Based Personalization},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2011},
  volume  = {30},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {170--186},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1100.0607}
}
```
An analytical model showing that when competing firms personalize offers based on customers' past behavior, they can intensify price competition and erode profits — personalization is not unambiguously beneficial to the firms that practice it. Zhang clarifies when forward-looking firms should restrain data-driven targeting. A core theory result on the strategic limits of behavior-based pricing.
Chapter: analytical-models

## Northwestern Kellogg — Consumer behavior (Lee, Rucker, Sternthal)

```bibtex
@article{aaker2001pleasures,
  author  = {Aaker, Jennifer L. and Lee, Angela Y.},
  title   = {``I'' Seek Pleasures and ``We'' Avoid Pains: The Role of Self-Regulatory Goals in Information Processing and Persuasion},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2001},
  volume  = {28},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {33--49},
  doi     = {10.1086/321946}
}
```
Demonstrates that self-construal (independent vs. interdependent) activates different regulatory goals — promotion vs. prevention — which in turn change which message appeals persuade and which information is recalled. Establishes the regulatory-focus-by-self-view interaction that underpins much of Lee's persuasion research. A foundational result connecting culture, motivation, and message framing.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{lee2006regulatoryfit,
  author  = {Aaker, Jennifer L. and Lee, Angela Y.},
  title   = {Understanding Regulatory Fit},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2006},
  volume  = {43},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {15--19},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.43.1.15}
}
```
A concise synthesis of regulatory fit — the idea that persuasion is enhanced when the manner of message processing matches the recipient's regulatory orientation — and its implications for marketing communication. Consolidates the construct and its boundary conditions for a marketing audience. A useful anchor for the message-framing and persuasion literature.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{rucker2008desire,
  author  = {Rucker, Derek D. and Galinsky, Adam D.},
  title   = {Desire to Acquire: Powerlessness and Compensatory Consumption},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {257--267},
  doi     = {10.1086/588569}
}
```
Shows that states of low power increase consumers' willingness to pay for and acquire status-signaling products as a way to compensate for the lacking sense of control — compensatory consumption. Rucker and Galinsky establish a robust, theory-driven link between psychological power and conspicuous spending. A foundational paper in the power-and-consumption literature.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{anand1990repetition,
  author  = {Anand, Punam and Sternthal, Brian},
  title   = {Ease of Message Processing as a Moderator of Repetition Effects in Advertising},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1990},
  volume  = {27},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {345--353},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224379002700308}
}
```
Tests the "two-factor" account of advertising repetition, showing that whether repeated exposure helps or wears out depends on how easy the message is to process: hard-to-process messages benefit from more repetition than easy ones. A classic Sternthal contribution on advertising wear-in and wear-out and the role of processing fluency.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{maheswaran1990knowledge,
  author  = {Maheswaran, Durairaj and Sternthal, Brian},
  title   = {The Effects of Knowledge, Motivation, and Type of Message on Ad Processing and Product Judgments},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {1990},
  volume  = {17},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {66--73},
  doi     = {10.1086/208537}
}
```
Shows that consumers' prior knowledge and motivation jointly determine whether attribute-focused or benefit-focused ad messages produce more favorable judgments — experts and novices respond to different message types. A foundational result on tailoring advertising content to audience expertise.
Chapter: advertising

## Northwestern Kellogg — Pricing, retail, and advertising structure (Anderson, Krishnamurthi, Tuchman)

```bibtex
@article{anderson2010stickiness,
  author  = {Anderson, Eric T. and Simester, Duncan I.},
  title   = {Price Stickiness and Customer Antagonism},
  journal = {Quarterly Journal of Economics},
  year    = {2010},
  volume  = {125},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {729--765},
  doi     = {10.1162/qjec.2010.125.2.729}
}
```
Combines a field experiment with theory to argue that firms keep prices sticky partly to avoid antagonizing customers, who react negatively to price increases they perceive as unfair. Anderson and Simester provide a behavioral microfoundation for nominal price rigidity grounded in fairness concerns. A bridge between marketing's fairness literature and macroeconomic price-stickiness.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{krishnamurthi1988brandchoice,
  author  = {Krishnamurthi, Lakshman and Raj, S. P.},
  title   = {A Model of Brand Choice and Purchase Quantity Price Sensitivities},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {1988},
  volume  = {7},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {1--20},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.7.1.1}
}
```
Develops an integrated model that separates how price affects which brand a household chooses from how much it buys, recognizing that the two decisions have distinct price sensitivities. An early and influential treatment of brand choice and purchase quantity in a unified econometric framework.
Chapter: empirical-models

```bibtex
@article{krishnamurthi1991loyalty,
  author  = {Krishnamurthi, Lakshman and Raj, S. P.},
  title   = {An Empirical Analysis of the Relationship Between Brand Loyalty and Consumer Price Elasticity},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {1991},
  volume  = {10},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {172--183},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.10.2.172}
}
```
Shows empirically that brand-loyal households are less price-sensitive in brand choice but can be more price-sensitive in purchase quantity — loyalty does not uniformly dampen price response. A foundational result connecting loyalty to differentiated pricing and promotion strategy.
Chapter: empirical-models

```bibtex
@article{tuchman2019ecig,
  author  = {Tuchman, Anna E.},
  title   = {Advertising and Demand for Addictive Goods: The Effects of E-Cigarette Advertising},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2019},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2019.1195}
  % Note: Crossref record is online-first (article-number mksc.2019.1195); no volume/issue/pages in version-of-record metadata as of 2026-06-21.
}
```
Estimates a structural demand model to ask whether banning television advertising for e-cigarettes would help or hurt public health, finding that such ads increase e-cigarette demand but also reduce cigarette demand via substitution — so a ban could have the unintended effect of raising traditional cigarette consumption. A frontier structural-empirical study of advertising for addictive goods with sharp policy stakes.
Chapter: structural-models

## Northwestern Kellogg — Statistical methods and inference (McShane)

```bibtex
@article{mcshane2016blinding,
  author  = {McShane, Blakeley B. and Gal, David},
  title   = {Blinding Us to the Obvious? The Effect of Statistical Training on the Evaluation of Evidence},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {62},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {1707--1718},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2015.2212}
}
```
Demonstrates experimentally that even expert researchers misread evidence when a p-value crosses the 0.05 threshold, dichotomizing continuous evidence into "effect" vs. "no effect." McShane and Gal show statistical training itself induces this bias. A pointed methodological contribution to how marketing and management scholars should interpret significance.
Chapter: metrics

```bibtex
@article{mcshane2017dichotomization,
  author  = {McShane, Blakeley B. and Gal, David},
  title   = {Statistical Significance and the Dichotomization of Evidence},
  journal = {Journal of the American Statistical Association},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {112},
  number  = {519},
  pages   = {885--895},
  doi     = {10.1080/01621459.2017.1289846}
}
```
Extends the prior finding into a broad critique of null-hypothesis significance testing, documenting how researchers across fields treat p < 0.05 as a bright line and proposing that evidence be evaluated continuously and holistically. A widely cited statement in the post-replication-crisis methods debate.
Chapter: metrics

```bibtex
@article{mcshane2019abandon,
  author  = {McShane, Blakeley B. and Gal, David and Gelman, Andrew and Robert, Christian and Tackett, Jennifer L.},
  title   = {Abandon Statistical Significance},
  journal = {The American Statistician},
  year    = {2019},
  volume  = {73},
  number  = {sup1},
  pages   = {235--245},
  doi     = {10.1080/00031305.2018.1527253}
}
```
Argues that the categorical use of statistical significance should be dropped entirely and p-values demoted to one piece of evidence among many, alongside prior knowledge, effect sizes, and study design. A high-profile, frontier position paper shaping how empirical marketing research reports and interprets results.
Chapter: metrics

## MIT Sloan — AI and consumer behavior (Gosline)

```bibtex
@article{zhang2023favoritism,
  author  = {Zhang, Yunhao and Gosline, Ren{\'e}e},
  title   = {Human Favoritism, Not AI Aversion: People's Perceptions (and Bias) Toward Generative AI, Human Experts, and Human--GAI Collaboration in Persuasive Content Generation},
  journal = {Judgment and Decision Making},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {18},
  pages   = {e41},
  doi     = {10.1017/jdm.2023.37}
}
```
Finds that when source is hidden, consumers rate generative-AI and AI-augmented persuasive content as higher quality than human-expert content; the penalty appears only when the human label is revealed — i.e., "human favoritism," not AI aversion, drives bias. A frontier study on how disclosure of AI authorship shapes perceived quality, directly relevant to AI-assisted marketing content.
Chapter: consumer-behavior
