# University of Michigan (Ross) — Verified Marketing Bibliography

Curated, **Crossref-verified** bibliography of influential and frontier marketing scholarship associated with the **University of Michigan, Ross School of Business**. Emphasis on sensory/consumer-behavior (Krishna lab), behavioral economics of spending (Rick/Burson), consumer neuroscience (Yoon), pharma/healthcare diffusion and structural models (Manchanda/Sriram), and the digital-advertising / experimentation frontier (Schwartz/Aribarg/Huang/Orhun/Feinberg).

**Verification protocol.** Every entry was matched against the Crossref REST API (`https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=...`) and each DOI was confirmed via `https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`. Title, author list, year, journal, volume/issue, and pages were checked against the returned version-of-record metadata. Published versions-of-record are preferred over SSRN/preprint DOIs (JM/JMR `10.1509/` and `10.1177/`; Marketing Science / Management Science `10.1287/`; JCR `10.1086/`; Elsevier JCPsy/JRetailing `10.1016/`; Springer `10.1007/`; Palgrave JIBS `10.1057/`; Wiley `10.1111/`). Items that could not be confirmed are listed at the bottom under **UNVERIFIED — do not cite**.

Already-gathered scholars (Tellis, Marinova, the conjoint set, branding-frontier and advertising sets) are not re-listed here. Note that several co-authors below (Chintagunta, Rossi, Steenkamp, Loewenstein, Bradlow, Fader, Narayanan) are not at Michigan; entries are included because the lead or anchor author is/was Ross faculty.

## Sensory Marketing & Consumer Behavior (Krishna lab)

```bibtex
@article{krishna2012sensory,
  author  = {Krishna, Aradhna},
  title   = {An Integrative Review of Sensory Marketing: Engaging the Senses to Affect Perception, Judgment and Behavior},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Psychology},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {22},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {332--351},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.003}
}
```
The field-defining integrative review that consolidated "sensory marketing" as a research program, defining it as marketing that engages the senses to affect perception, judgment, and behavior. Organizes the literature by sense (vision, audition, smell, touch, taste) and links sensory cues to grounded cognition and largely subconscious processing. The standard framing citation for the sensory chapter.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{krishna2014embodiment,
  author  = {Krishna, Aradhna and Schwarz, Norbert},
  title   = {Sensory Marketing, Embodiment, and Grounded Cognition: A Review and Introduction},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Psychology},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {24},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {159--168},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.jcps.2013.12.006}
}
```
A theory-forward review tying sensory marketing to embodiment and grounded-cognition accounts, arguing that mental representations are grounded in sensory and motor experience rather than abstract symbols. Synthesizes evidence that bodily and perceptual states shape judgment, providing the cognitive-science scaffolding for sensory-marketing effects. Useful as the conceptual companion to Krishna (2012).
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{krishna2008touch,
  author  = {Krishna, Aradhna and Morrin, Maureen},
  title   = {Does Touch Affect Taste? The Perceptual Transfer of Product Container Haptic Cues},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {34},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {807--818},
  doi     = {10.1086/523286}
}
```
Experimental demonstration that haptic (touch) cues from a product's container transfer perceptually to taste evaluations: water in a flimsy cup is judged worse than identical water in a firm cup, but only among consumers low in autotelic need-for-touch. Establishes cross-modal "perceptual transfer" as a mechanism by which non-diagnostic sensory cues bias product judgments. Method: controlled lab taste-test experiments with measured individual differences.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{cian2015dynamic,
  author  = {Cian, Luca and Krishna, Aradhna and Elder, Ryan S.},
  title   = {A Sign of Things to Come: Behavioral Change through Dynamic Iconography},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {41},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {1426--1446},
  doi     = {10.1086/680673}
}
```
Shows that static images conveying implied motion ("dynamic imagery," e.g., a more dynamic stick-figure crossing sign) increase perceived movement, attract attention, and change real behavior. Connects visual depiction to engagement and downstream action, with implications for warning signs, logos, and persuasive imagery. Method: multi-study experiments combining perception ratings with behavioral measures.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{krishna2017package,
  author  = {Krishna, Aradhna and Cian, Luca and Aydino\u{g}lu, Nil\"{u}fer Z.},
  title   = {Sensory Aspects of Package Design},
  journal = {Journal of Retailing},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {93},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {43--54},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.jretai.2016.12.002}
}
```
A focused review of how package design engages each sense — visual (color, shape, imagery), tactile, auditory, and olfactory — to shape product perception and choice at the shelf. Synthesizes the sensory-marketing literature into actionable design dimensions for retailers and brand managers. Useful bridge between the sensory and branding chapters.
Chapter: branding

## Behavioral Economics of Spending & Judgment (Rick / Burson)

```bibtex
@article{rick2008tightwads,
  author  = {Rick, Scott I. and Cryder, Cynthia E. and Loewenstein, George},
  title   = {Tightwads and Spendthrifts},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {34},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {767--782},
  doi     = {10.1086/523285}
}
```
Introduces and validates the "Spendthrift–Tightwad" scale, showing that chronic individual differences in the anticipatory "pain of paying" — not just attitudes or income — drive spending. Tightwads spend less than they ideally would and respond strongly to pain-reducing framings (e.g., bundling, flat fees). Method: scale development plus large-sample survey and field validation; a foundational reference for the psychology of spending.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{rick2014retailtherapy,
  author  = {Rick, Scott I. and Pereira, Beatriz and Burson, Katherine A.},
  title   = {The Benefits of Retail Therapy: Making Purchase Decisions Reduces Residual Sadness},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Psychology},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {24},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {373--380},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.jcps.2013.12.004}
}
```
Tests "retail therapy" experimentally: making shopping choices — even hypothetical ones — alleviates residual sadness, with the effect mediated by restored personal control rather than mood repair through acquisition. A clean demonstration linking emotion regulation to consumer decision-making (dual Michigan authorship, Rick and Burson). Method: lab experiments inducing sadness and manipulating choice.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{burson2009sixofone,
  author  = {Burson, Katherine A. and Larrick, Richard P. and Lynch, Jr., John G.},
  title   = {Six of One, Half Dozen of the Other: Expanding and Contracting Numerical Dimensions Produces Preference Reversals},
  journal = {Psychological Science},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {20},
  number  = {9},
  pages   = {1074--1078},
  doi     = {10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02394.x}
}
```
Demonstrates "unit effects": expanding a numerical scale (e.g., price per year vs. per month, or coverage in days vs. years) makes attribute differences loom larger and reverses preferences, because consumers attend to numerosity rather than rescaling. Directly relevant to pricing presentation, metric framing, and how attribute units are communicated. Method: multiple choice experiments with manipulated scale granularity.
Chapter: pricing

## Consumer Neuroscience (Yoon)

```bibtex
@article{yoon2006fmri,
  author  = {Yoon, Carolyn and Gutchess, Angela H. and Feinberg, Fred and Polk, Thad A.},
  title   = {A Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging Study of Neural Dissociations between Brand and Person Judgments},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2006},
  volume  = {33},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {31--40},
  doi     = {10.1086/504132}
}
```
An early and influential consumer-neuroscience study showing that judging brands recruits neural regions associated with object/semantic processing rather than the person-perception network used for people. Provides fMRI evidence against the "brands-as-people" metaphor at the neural level, helping establish consumer neuroscience as a method. Method: within-subject fMRI comparing brand- vs. person-trait judgments.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{yoon2012decisionneuro,
  author  = {Yoon, Carolyn and Gonzalez, Richard and Bechara, Antoine and Berns, Gregory S. and Dagher, Alain A. and Dub\'{e}, Laurette and Huettel, Scott A. and Kable, Joseph W. and Liberzon, Israel and Plassmann, Hilke and Smidts, Ale and Spence, Charles},
  title   = {Decision Neuroscience and Consumer Decision Making},
  journal = {Marketing Letters},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {23},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {473--485},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11002-012-9188-z}
}
```
A multi-author agenda-setting review (led by Yoon) mapping how decision-neuroscience methods and findings inform consumer decision-making research, covering valuation, self-control, social influence, and methodological cautions. The standard orientation reference for marketing scholars entering neuroscience. Method: conceptual review and research agenda.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Pharmaceutical / Healthcare Diffusion & Structural Models (Manchanda / Sriram)

```bibtex
@article{manchanda2004response,
  author  = {Manchanda, Puneet and Rossi, Peter E. and Chintagunta, Pradeep K.},
  title   = {Response Modeling with Nonrandom Marketing-Mix Variables},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {41},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {467--478},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.41.4.467.47005}
}
```
Addresses endogeneity of marketing-mix variables (here, pharmaceutical detailing) by jointly modeling firm targeting decisions and physician response in a Bayesian framework, correcting biased response estimates that arise when marketing effort is set non-randomly. A methodological cornerstone for response models with endogenous marketing actions. Method: hierarchical Bayesian simultaneous-equation model on physician panel data.
Chapter: empirical-models

```bibtex
@article{manchanda2008contagion,
  author  = {Manchanda, Puneet and Xie, Ying and Youn, Nara},
  title   = {The Role of Targeted Communication and Contagion in Product Adoption},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {27},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {961--976},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1070.0354}
}
```
Decomposes new-product (prescription drug) adoption into firm-initiated targeted communication versus peer contagion among physicians, estimating a hazard model on individual adoption timing. Finds both channels matter and quantifies a social multiplier on marketing spend. A key reference for diffusion, social contagion, and the economics of word-of-mouth in professional networks. Method: individual-level hazard model with contagion terms.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{chu2016network,
  author  = {Chu, Junhong and Manchanda, Puneet},
  title   = {Quantifying Cross and Direct Network Effects in Online Consumer-to-Consumer Platforms},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {870--893},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2016.0976}
}
```
Estimates direct and cross-side network effects on a large C2C platform (Taobao), showing how growth in buyers and sellers reinforce each other and how the installed base on one side propels the other. Foundational empirical evidence for two-sided-market dynamics in marketing. Method: dynamic structural / reduced-form estimation on large-scale platform panel data.
Chapter: strategic-dynamic

```bibtex
@article{sriram2015variability,
  author  = {Sriram, S. and Chintagunta, Pradeep K. and Manchanda, Puneet},
  title   = {Service Quality Variability and Termination Behavior},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {61},
  number  = {11},
  pages   = {2739--2759},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2014.2105}
}
```
Shows that variability in service quality — not just its average level — drives customer churn, and that the retention payoff to raising average quality is much larger for households experiencing low variability. Connects experience consistency to customer lifetime value and retention strategy. Method: structural model of termination on telecom subscriber panel data.
Chapter: clv

## Digital Advertising, Experimentation & Choice Frontier (Schwartz / Aribarg / Huang / Orhun / Feinberg)

```bibtex
@article{schwartz2017bandit,
  author  = {Schwartz, Eric M. and Bradlow, Eric T. and Fader, Peter S.},
  title   = {Customer Acquisition via Display Advertising Using Multi-Armed Bandit Experiments},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {500--522},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2016.1023}
}
```
Brings multi-armed bandit methods to live display-advertising campaigns, formalizing the "learn-and-earn" trade-off between exploring ad/audience combinations and exploiting the best ones. A field deployment over 750M+ impressions improved customer acquisition versus standard practice. A frontier reference for adaptive experimentation and online learning in marketing. Method: Bayesian multivariate bandit with a randomized field experiment.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{aribarg2020native,
  author  = {Aribarg, Anocha and Schwartz, Eric M.},
  title   = {Native Advertising in Online News: Trade-Offs Among Clicks, Brand Recognition, and Website Trustworthiness},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2020},
  volume  = {57},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {20--34},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022243719879711}
}
```
Quantifies the trade-offs of native (in-feed, editorial-looking) ads versus display ads: native formats can win more clicks but erode brand recognition and perceived website trustworthiness once disclosure is salient. Important for the ethics-and-effectiveness debate around disguised advertising. Method: randomized experiments combining click behavior, eye-tracking, and survey measures.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{braun2025abtesting,
  author  = {Braun, Michael and Schwartz, Eric M.},
  title   = {Where {A/B} Testing Goes Wrong: How Divergent Delivery Affects What Online Experiments Cannot (and Can) Tell You About How Customers Respond to Advertising},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2025},
  volume  = {89},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {71--95},
  doi     = {10.1177/00222429241275886}
}
```
Shows that platform ad-delivery algorithms cause "divergent delivery" — the test and control arms of an A/B test reach systematically different users — confounding ad-content effects with algorithmic targeting and invalidating naive experimental comparisons. A frontier critique essential for anyone interpreting online advertising experiments. Method: analytical framework plus simulation and empirical illustration.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{orhun2022goldstatus,
  author  = {Orhun, A. Ye\c{s}im and Guo, Tong and Hagemann, Andreas},
  title   = {Reaching for Gold: Frequent-Flyer Status Incentives and Moral Hazard},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2022},
  volume  = {41},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {548--574},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2021.1341}
}
```
Documents goal-gradient and moral-hazard behavior in a loyalty program: as members approach an elite-status threshold they accelerate qualifying purchases, and the incentive design reshapes spending in ways with welfare and profitability consequences. A clean empirical study of loyalty-program incentive structure. Method: large-scale loyalty-program data with quasi-experimental identification.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{huang2026novelty,
  author  = {Huang, Justin and Kaul, Rupali and Narayanan, Sridhar},
  title   = {Novelty in Content Creation: Experimental Results Using Deep Learning--Based Image Recognition on a Large Social Network},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2026},
  volume  = {45},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {335--358},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2023.0547}
}
```
Combines a large-scale field experiment on a social network with deep-learning image recognition to measure whether attention and recognition cause creators to produce more (or less) novel content. A frontier example of ML-based measurement of unstructured content plus causal field experimentation. Method: randomized field experiment with CNN-based novelty measurement of images.
Chapter: data

```bibtex
@article{aribarg2010jointchoice,
  author  = {Aribarg, Anocha and Arora, Neeraj and Kang, Moon Young},
  title   = {Predicting Joint Choice Using Individual Data},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2010},
  volume  = {29},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {139--157},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1090.0490}
}
```
Develops a model that predicts couples' joint (group) choices from individually elicited preferences, capturing influence and compromise in dyadic decision-making. Relevant to preference measurement and conjoint when the buying unit is a household rather than an individual. Method: Bayesian model of preference revision estimated on individual and joint conjoint data.
Chapter: preference-measurement

```bibtex
@article{salisbury2012choiceset,
  author  = {Salisbury, Linda Court and Feinberg, Fred M.},
  title   = {All Things Considered? The Role of Choice Set Formation in Diversification},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {49},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {320--335},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.10.0437}
}
```
Examines variety-seeking and "diversification bias," showing that how the choice set is formed and considered shapes how much variety consumers build into simultaneous (vs. sequential) choices. A behaviorally grounded choice-modeling contribution (Feinberg) on consideration-set formation. Method: choice experiments with formal modeling of set formation.
Chapter: modeling

## Global Branding (Batra)

```bibtex
@article{steenkamp2003globalness,
  author  = {Steenkamp, Jan-Benedict E. M. and Batra, Rajeev and Alden, Dana L.},
  title   = {How Perceived Brand Globalness Creates Brand Value},
  journal = {Journal of International Business Studies},
  year    = {2003},
  volume  = {34},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {53--65},
  doi     = {10.1057/palgrave.jibs.8400002}
}
```
Shows that perceived brand globalness raises perceived brand quality and prestige, which in turn increase purchase likelihood — a mechanism distinct from objective globalness. A foundational reference for global branding strategy and the value of "global" positioning. Method: consumer survey with structural-equation modeling across product categories.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{batra2012brandlove,
  author  = {Batra, Rajeev and Ahuvia, Aaron and Bagozzi, Richard P.},
  title   = {Brand Love},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {76},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {1--16},
  doi     = {10.1509/jm.09.0339}
}
```
Develops a grounded, consumer-derived model of "brand love" comprising elements such as self–brand integration, passion-driven behaviors, positive emotional connection, long-term relationship orientation, and anticipated separation distress. Shifts brand attachment from a single attitude to a richer relational construct predicting loyalty and word-of-mouth. Method: qualitative theory-building followed by quantitative scale validation.
Chapter: branding
