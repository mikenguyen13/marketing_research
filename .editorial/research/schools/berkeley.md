# UC Berkeley (Haas School of Business) — Marketing Scholarship

Verified bibliography of influential and frontier marketing scholarship associated with the Haas School of Business at the University of California, Berkeley. Every entry below has a DOI confirmed against Crossref via both the bibliographic query endpoint and the `works/<doi>` record. All DOIs are published versions of record (no SSRN/preprints). Items co-authored with non-Haas collaborators are retained where a Haas marketing faculty member is a principal author.

Faculty covered: J. Miguel Villas-Boas (theory/dynamics/learning/privacy), Ganesh Iyer (theory/channels/advertising/persuasion), Przemyslaw Jeziorski (structural IO/radio/mobile/auctions), Zsolt Katona (networks/social/diffusion), Clayton Critcher (consumer judgment), Leif Nelson (CB; false-positive/replication methods), Ming Hsu (consumer neuroscience).

Notes on scope: Per editorial instruction, scholars already gathered elsewhere (Tellis, Marinova, conjoint set, branding-frontier and advertising sets incl. Blake/Nosko/Tadelis) are not re-listed here.

## Analytical Models — Theory & Dynamics (Villas-Boas)

```bibtex
@article{villasboas2004learning,
  author  = {Villas-Boas, J. Miguel},
  title   = {Consumer Learning, Brand Loyalty, and Competition},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {23},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {134--145},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1030.0044}
}
```
Builds a dynamic duopoly in which consumers learn the fit of a product over time, generating endogenous brand loyalty. A key result is that forward-looking marginal consumers can be less price sensitive than myopic ones, reshaping equilibrium pricing relative to static models. A foundational theory reference for consumer learning and state-dependent demand.
**Chapter:** analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{villasboas2009productvariety,
  author  = {Villas-Boas, J. Miguel},
  title   = {Product Variety and Endogenous Pricing with Evaluation Costs},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {55},
  number  = {8},
  pages   = {1338--1346},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.1090.1024}
}
```
Examines optimal product-line length when consumers incur costs to evaluate options. Shows that offering fewer products can serve as a commitment device not to extract all consumer surplus, trading off better fit against the disincentive that high prices create for costly evaluation. Central to assortment and choice-architecture theory.
**Chapter:** analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{villasboas2004pricecycles,
  author  = {Villas-Boas, J. Miguel},
  title   = {Price Cycles in Markets with Customer Recognition},
  journal = {The RAND Journal of Economics},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {486--501},
  doi     = {10.2307/1593704}
}
```
Models markets where firms can recognize and target previously acquired customers, producing endogenous price cycles over the customer relationship. An early formal treatment of behavior-based price discrimination and customer recognition that anticipates much of the later privacy-and-targeting literature.
**Chapter:** pricing

## Privacy & Behavior-Based Targeting (Villas-Boas)

```bibtex
@article{shen2018behaviorbased,
  author  = {Shen, Qiaowei and Villas-Boas, J. Miguel},
  title   = {Behavior-Based Advertising},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {64},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {2047--2064},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2016.2719}
}
```
Analyzes firms that use observed past behavior to target advertising, characterizing when behavior-based advertising intensifies or softens competition. Connects the economics of consumer tracking to advertising strategy, making it a strong frontier reference for the privacy/targeting interface.
**Chapter:** privacy

## Analytical Models — Channels, Advertising & Persuasion (Iyer)

```bibtex
@article{iyer1998channels,
  author  = {Iyer, Ganesh},
  title   = {Coordinating Channels Under Price and Nonprice Competition},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {1998},
  volume  = {17},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {338--355},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.17.4.338}
}
```
Studies how a manufacturer designs distribution to coordinate retailers competing on both price and non-price (service) dimensions. Shows that the optimal degree of retail competition differs across these dimensions, a foundational result for channel and distribution theory.
**Chapter:** analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{iyer2005targeting,
  author  = {Iyer, Ganesh and Soberman, David and Villas-Boas, J. Miguel},
  title   = {The Targeting of Advertising},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2005},
  volume  = {24},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {461--476},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1050.0117}
}
```
Develops a model of targeted advertising in which firms direct messages toward loyal segments rather than comparison shoppers. Advertising less to switchers endogenously increases differentiation and softens price competition, an influential result for the economics of targeting.
**Chapter:** advertising

```bibtex
@article{iyer2012shopping,
  author  = {Iyer, Ganesh and Kuksov, Dmitri},
  title   = {Competition in Consumer Shopping Experience},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {31},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {913--933},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1120.0734}
}
```
Models the strategic provision of in-store/shopping experience as a competitive instrument alongside price. Clarifies when investing in experience is an effective differentiator versus when it triggers escalating competition, relevant to experiential retail and store atmospherics.
**Chapter:** analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{iyer2022persuasion,
  author  = {Iyer, Ganesh and Singh, Shubhranshu},
  title   = {Persuasion Contest: Disclosing Own and Rival Information},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2022},
  volume  = {41},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {682--709},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2021.1333}
}
```
Models a contest in which firms persuade consumers by strategically disclosing their own and rivals' information. Extends Bayesian-persuasion and information-design ideas to competitive marketing settings, a frontier analytical contribution to advertising-as-information.
**Chapter:** advertising

## Networks, Diffusion & Influence (Katona)

```bibtex
@article{katona2008networkformation,
  author  = {Katona, Zsolt and Sarvary, Miklos},
  title   = {Network Formation and the Structure of the Commercial World Wide Web},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {27},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {764--778},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1070.0349}
}
```
Models the commercial web as an equilibrium network where sites buy advertising links and set prices, explaining observed specialization between content-rich and traffic-aggregating sites. An early structural account of link economics with implications for online advertising and search.
**Chapter:** analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{katona2011networkeffects,
  author  = {Katona, Zsolt and Zubcsek, Peter Pal and Sarvary, Miklos},
  title   = {Network Effects and Personal Influences: The Diffusion of an Online Social Network},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2011},
  volume  = {48},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {425--443},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.48.3.425}
}
```
Uses adoption data from an online social network to separate structural network effects from personal influence, identifying distinct "degree" and "clustering" effects on adoption. Empirically grounds influencer and seeding strategy by showing how local network position shapes contagion.
**Chapter:** virality

## Structural Models & Industrial Organization (Jeziorski)

```bibtex
@article{jeziorski2014twosided,
  author  = {Jeziorski, Przemyslaw},
  title   = {Effects of Mergers in Two-Sided Markets: The US Radio Industry},
  journal = {American Economic Journal: Microeconomics},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {6},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {35--73},
  doi     = {10.1257/mic.6.4.35}
}
```
Estimates a structural model of the US radio industry as a two-sided market linking listeners and advertisers, then simulates merger effects. Finds conflicting incentives to exercise market power across the two sides, a methodological touchstone for media-market and platform analysis.
**Chapter:** structural-models

```bibtex
@article{jeziorski2014costefficiencies,
  author  = {Jeziorski, Przemyslaw},
  title   = {Estimation of Cost Efficiencies from Mergers: Application to US Radio},
  journal = {The RAND Journal of Economics},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {45},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {816--846},
  doi     = {10.1111/1756-2171.12072}
}
```
Companion structural study recovering cost synergies from radio-industry mergers and weighing them against market-power effects. Demonstrates how merger simulation can disentangle efficiency gains from competitive harm in concentrated media markets.
**Chapter:** industrial-organization

```bibtex
@article{economides2017mobilemoney,
  author  = {Economides, Nicholas and Jeziorski, Przemyslaw},
  title   = {Mobile Money in Tanzania},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {815--837},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2017.1027}
}
```
Uses transaction-level data to structurally estimate demand for mobile-money services in Tanzania, quantifying consumers' willingness to pay to avoid carrying cash and to store value. A frontier empirical study of fintech adoption and financial inclusion in developing markets.
**Chapter:** structural-models

```bibtex
@article{jeziorski2019skimming,
  author  = {Jeziorski, Przemyslaw and Krasnokutskaya, Elena and Ceccarini, Olivia},
  title   = {Skimming from the Bottom: Empirical Evidence of Adverse Selection When Poaching Customers},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2019},
  volume  = {38},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {543--566},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2018.1134}
}
```
Provides empirical evidence of adverse selection in competitive customer poaching, where firms attracting rivals' customers disproportionately draw costly-to-serve ones. Links behavior-based targeting to selection economics, bridging the privacy/targeting and structural literatures.
**Chapter:** structural-models

```bibtex
@article{jeziorski2023merger,
  author  = {Jeziorski, Przemyslaw},
  title   = {Empirical Model of Dynamic Merger Enforcement---Choosing Ownership Caps in U.S. Radio},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {69},
  number  = {8},
  pages   = {4457--4480},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2022.4599}
}
```
Builds a dynamic oligopoly model with endogenous mergers, entry, and exit to evaluate ownership-cap policy in US radio. A recent frontier contribution showing how dynamic structural models can inform antitrust and regulatory design.
**Chapter:** industrial-organization

## Consumer Behavior & Judgment (Critcher, Nelson)

```bibtex
@article{odonnell2023bundle,
  author  = {O'Donnell, Michael and Critcher, Clayton R. and Nelson, Leif D.},
  title   = {Bundle Selection and Variety Seeking: The Importance of Combinatorics},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {49},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {861--881},
  doi     = {10.1093/jcr/ucac017}
}
```
Shows that consumers building bundles seek more variety than is normatively warranted because they misweight the combinatorics of how items can be experienced. A behavioral-judgment contribution with direct implications for assortment design and choice architecture.
**Chapter:** consumer-behavior

## Replication Methods & Research Integrity (Nelson)

```bibtex
@article{simmons2011falsepositive,
  author  = {Simmons, Joseph P. and Nelson, Leif D. and Simonsohn, Uri},
  title   = {False-Positive Psychology: Undisclosed Flexibility in Data Collection and Analysis Allows Presenting Anything as Significant},
  journal = {Psychological Science},
  year    = {2011},
  volume  = {22},
  number  = {11},
  pages   = {1359--1366},
  doi     = {10.1177/0956797611417632}
}
```
The landmark demonstration that "researcher degrees of freedom" can push false-positive rates far above nominal levels, catalyzing the credibility/replication movement across behavioral science and consumer research. Required reading for empirical methodology and pre-registration discussions.
**Chapter:** empirical-models

```bibtex
@article{simonsohn2014pcurve,
  author  = {Simonsohn, Uri and Nelson, Leif D. and Simmons, Joseph P.},
  title   = {P-Curve: A Key to the File-Drawer},
  journal = {Journal of Experimental Psychology: General},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {143},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {534--547},
  doi     = {10.1037/a0033242}
}
```
Introduces p-curve, a tool that uses the distribution of significant p-values to diagnose whether a literature reflects genuine effects or selective reporting / p-hacking. A core methodological contribution for evaluating evidential value in marketing meta-analysis.
**Chapter:** empirical-models

## Consumer Neuroscience (Hsu, Nelson)

```bibtex
@article{chen2015brandbrain,
  author  = {Chen, Yu-Ping and Nelson, Leif D. and Hsu, Ming},
  title   = {From "Where" to "What": Distributed Representations of Brand Associations in the Human Brain},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {52},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {453--466},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.14.0606}
}
```
Combines neuroimaging with machine learning to show that brand-personality associations are encoded in distributed patterns of brain activity across reasoning, imagery, and affective regions. A frontier consumer-neuroscience study linking branding constructs to neural representation.
**Chapter:** branding

```bibtex
@article{hsu2017neuromarketing,
  author  = {Hsu, Ming},
  title   = {Neuromarketing: Inside the Mind of the Consumer},
  journal = {California Management Review},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {59},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {5--22},
  doi     = {10.1177/0008125617720208}
}
```
A managerial overview of how brain-based methods can complement traditional consumer research, with guidance on where neuromarketing adds incremental insight and where it does not. Useful as an accessible framing reference for the consumer-neuroscience chapter.
**Chapter:** consumer-behavior
