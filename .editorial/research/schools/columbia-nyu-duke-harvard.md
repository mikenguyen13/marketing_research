# Columbia, NYU Stern, Duke Fuqua & Harvard Business School — Verified Bibliography

Curated bibliography of influential and frontier marketing scholarship associated with **Columbia Business School**, **NYU Stern School of Business**, **Duke University Fuqua School of Business**, and **Harvard Business School**, mapped to book chapters. The set spans the structural/empirical-modeling traditions (Jedidi, Ansari, Ghose), the consumer-behavior and judgment-and-decision-making schools (Pham, Johar, Meyvis, Raghubir, Bettman, Luce, Fitzsimons, Alter, Norton, Ariely, Gourville), the experiential/branding stream (Schmitt), the digital/platform-advertising stream (Jerath, Ghose), the marketing-strategy/CMO stream (Moorman), and the entertainment/customer-equity streams (Elberse, Gupta, Lehmann, Teixeira).

**Verification protocol.** Every entry below was matched against the Crossref REST API (`https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=...`) and the DOI was resolved against the version-of-record metadata. Each DOI, title, author list, year, container-title (journal), volume, issue, and page range was checked against the returned record. Published versions-of-record are used in preference to any SSRN / NBER / working-paper DOI; where Crossref returned only a preprint DOI, the published DOI was located by a follow-up query and substituted. Items that could not be verified to a published DOI are quarantined at the bottom under **UNVERIFIED — do not cite**.

Scholars already gathered elsewhere in the research set (Oded Netzer, Olivier Toubia, Carl Mela, Wagner Kamakura, Tellis, Marinova, and the conjoint, branding-frontier, and advertising sets) are not re-listed here as primary entries, though they appear as co-authors where the lead author belongs to one of these four schools.

## Columbia Business School

```bibtex
@article{jedidi1997finite,
  author  = {Jedidi, Kamel and Jagpal, Harsharanjeet S. and DeSarbo, Wayne S.},
  title   = {Finite-Mixture Structural Equation Models for Response-Based Segmentation and Unobserved Heterogeneity},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {1997},
  volume  = {16},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {39--59},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.16.1.39}
}
```
Introduces finite-mixture structural equation models that simultaneously estimate latent-variable relationships and recover unobserved customer segments from response data. The method lets researchers detect heterogeneity in the structural model itself rather than assuming a single homogeneous population, a recurring problem in survey-based marketing research. A foundational reference for response-based segmentation that combines measurement, structure, and clustering in one estimator.
Chapter: structural-models

```bibtex
@article{ansari2000hierarchical,
  author  = {Ansari, Asim and Jedidi, Kamel and Jagpal, Sharan},
  title   = {A Hierarchical Bayesian Methodology for Treating Heterogeneity in Structural Equation Models},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2000},
  volume  = {19},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {328--347},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.19.4.328.11789}
}
```
Develops a hierarchical Bayesian approach to structural equation modeling that treats both continuous and discrete forms of consumer heterogeneity through random effects rather than a small number of discrete segments. The framework yields individual-level parameter estimates and full posterior uncertainty, advancing beyond fixed-coefficient SEM. A methodological bridge between Bayesian hierarchical modeling and the latent-variable measurement tradition.
Chapter: structural-models

```bibtex
@article{ansari2003ecustomization,
  author  = {Ansari, Asim and Mela, Carl F.},
  title   = {E-Customization},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2003},
  volume  = {40},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {131--145},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.40.2.131.19224}
}
```
Shows how a firm can use customer-level data to design and target the content and layout of email and web communications, optimizing what each customer sees to maximize engagement. The paper formalizes statistical personalization of marketing communications well before "recommendation systems" became standard, combining design optimization with individual-level response modeling. An early and influential treatment of data-driven one-to-one marketing.
Chapter: data

```bibtex
@article{ansari2008channel,
  author  = {Ansari, Asim and Mela, Carl F. and Neslin, Scott A.},
  title   = {Customer Channel Migration},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {45},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {60--76},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.45.1.60}
}
```
Models how customers move across purchase channels (e.g., from offline to the internet) over time and how firms can profitably influence that migration through targeted interventions. Using individual-level longitudinal data, the authors quantify the long-run value consequences of shifting customers toward lower-cost channels. A core reference for multichannel customer management and channel-mix strategy.
Chapter: clv

```bibtex
@article{pham1998representativeness,
  author  = {Pham, Michel Tuan},
  title   = {Representativeness, Relevance, and the Use of Feelings in Decision Making},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {1998},
  volume  = {25},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {144--159},
  doi     = {10.1086/209532}
}
```
Argues that consumers rely on their feelings as inputs to judgment to the degree those feelings seem representative of, and relevant to, the decision at hand. The work helped establish the "feelings-as-information" program in consumer research and specifies the conditions under which affect is and is not used. A seminal statement on the role of affect in consumer decision making.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{pham2001affect,
  author  = {Pham, Michel Tuan and Cohen, Joel B. and Pracejus, John W. and Hughes, G. David},
  title   = {Affect Monitoring and the Primacy of Feelings in Judgment},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2001},
  volume  = {28},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {167--188},
  doi     = {10.1086/322896}
}
```
Demonstrates that affective reactions to a target can be accessed faster than descriptive or evaluative judgments, supporting a "primacy of affect" view of consumer response. The authors introduce affect-monitoring tasks to show feelings arise early and can anchor subsequent deliberation. Important evidence for the speed and automaticity of emotional responses in consumer judgment.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{johar2006mapping,
  author  = {Johar, Gita Venkataramani and Maheswaran, Durairaj and Peracchio, Laura A.},
  title   = {{MAP}ping the Frontiers: Theoretical Advances in Consumer Research on Memory, Affect, and Persuasion},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2006},
  volume  = {33},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {139--149},
  doi     = {10.1086/500493}
}
```
A synthesizing review that maps theoretical advances across three pillars of consumer psychology — memory, affect, and persuasion — and identifies open frontiers for integration. Johar and co-authors clarify how persuasion outcomes depend jointly on what consumers remember and how they feel. A useful orienting reference for the consumer-behavior and communication literatures.
Chapter: communication

```bibtex
@article{schmitt1999experiential,
  author  = {Schmitt, Bernd},
  title   = {Experiential Marketing},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Management},
  year    = {1999},
  volume  = {15},
  number  = {1--3},
  pages   = {53--67},
  doi     = {10.1362/026725799784870496}
}
```
Lays out the experiential-marketing framework, arguing that firms should manage holistic customer experiences — sense, feel, think, act, and relate — rather than focus narrowly on features and benefits. Schmitt's "strategic experiential modules" became a widely cited vocabulary for experience-centric branding and retail design. A foundational statement of the experiential turn in marketing.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{jerath2014click,
  author  = {Jerath, Kinshuk and Ma, Liye and Park, Young-Hoon},
  title   = {Consumer Click Behavior at a Search Engine: The Role of Keyword Popularity},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {51},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {480--486},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.13.0099}
}
```
Analyzes how the popularity of a search keyword shapes consumers' click behavior on organic and sponsored results, with direct implications for search-advertising strategy. The authors show systematic differences in click patterns between popular and niche keywords that affect advertiser returns. A concise, data-grounded contribution to the economics of search-engine advertising.
Chapter: advertising

## NYU Stern School of Business

```bibtex
@article{morwitz2007intentions,
  author  = {Morwitz, Vicki G. and Steckel, Joel H. and Gupta, Alok},
  title   = {When Do Purchase Intentions Predict Sales?},
  journal = {International Journal of Forecasting},
  year    = {2007},
  volume  = {23},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {347--364},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.ijforecast.2007.05.015}
}
```
Asks the practical question of when stated purchase intentions are good predictors of actual sales, and identifies the conditions (e.g., existing vs. new products, durable vs. nondurable) under which the intention-behavior link strengthens or weakens. The paper gives forecasters concrete guidance on the validity of intentions data. A definitive reference on purchase-intention measurement and prediction.
Chapter: preference-measurement

```bibtex
@article{meyvis2002beliefs,
  author  = {Meyvis, Tom and Janiszewski, Chris},
  title   = {Consumers' Beliefs about Product Benefits: The Effect of Obviously Irrelevant Product Information},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2002},
  volume  = {28},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {618--635},
  doi     = {10.1086/338205}
}
```
Shows that adding obviously irrelevant information to a product description can dilute consumers' beliefs about the product's key benefits, weakening rather than strengthening persuasion. The dilution effect highlights how consumers integrate information non-normatively when forming benefit judgments. An important caution about the costs of cluttered messaging.
Chapter: communication

```bibtex
@article{meyvis2004brand,
  author  = {Meyvis, Tom and Janiszewski, Chris},
  title   = {When Are Broader Brands Stronger Brands? An Accessibility Perspective on the Success of Brand Extensions},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {31},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {346--357},
  doi     = {10.1086/422113}
}
```
Uses an accessibility framework to explain when extending a brand across many categories helps versus hurts the parent brand and its new extensions. The authors show that broader brands can be stronger when the brand's benefit associations remain accessible and relevant in the new context. A theoretically grounded account of brand-extension success.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{raghubir2009denomination,
  author  = {Raghubir, Priya and Srivastava, Joydeep},
  title   = {The Denomination Effect},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {701--713},
  doi     = {10.1086/599222}
}
```
Documents the "denomination effect": consumers are more likely to spend money held in small denominations than the equivalent value held in a single large bill, because large denominations are psychologically harder to break. The finding links the physical form of money to spending self-control. A landmark result in the psychology of money and consumer spending.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{ghose2011mobile,
  author  = {Ghose, Anindya and Han, Sang Pil},
  title   = {An Empirical Analysis of User Content Generation and Usage Behavior on the Mobile Internet},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2011},
  volume  = {57},
  number  = {9},
  pages   = {1671--1691},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.1110.1350}
}
```
Provides one of the first large-scale empirical analyses of how consumers generate and consume content on the mobile internet, distinguishing mobile usage from fixed-line internet behavior. Ghose and Han quantify the interplay between content generation and consumption and the role of network and device factors. A foundational empirical study of mobile consumer behavior.
Chapter: data

```bibtex
@article{ghose2014apps,
  author  = {Ghose, Anindya and Han, Sang Pil},
  title   = {Estimating Demand for Mobile Applications in the New Economy},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {60},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {1470--1488},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2014.1945}
}
```
Builds a structural demand model for mobile applications, estimating how price, rank, in-app features, and user reviews drive app demand in a fast-moving platform market. The framework supports counterfactual analysis of pricing and design decisions in app ecosystems. A key structural treatment of demand in digital-platform markets.
Chapter: structural-models

```bibtex
@article{alter2006fluency,
  author  = {Alter, Adam L. and Oppenheimer, Daniel M.},
  title   = {Predicting Short-Term Stock Fluctuations by Using Processing Fluency},
  journal = {Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences},
  year    = {2006},
  volume  = {103},
  number  = {24},
  pages   = {9369--9372},
  doi     = {10.1073/pnas.0601071103}

```
Demonstrates that stocks with more fluently processed names and ticker symbols (easier to pronounce) outperform less fluent ones shortly after issuance. The result is a striking real-world demonstration that processing fluency — a low-level metacognitive cue — biases consequential financial judgments. A widely cited bridge between metacognition research and market behavior.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Duke University Fuqua School of Business

```bibtex
@article{bettman1998constructive,
  author  = {Bettman, James R. and Luce, Mary Frances and Payne, John W.},
  title   = {Constructive Consumer Choice Processes},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {1998},
  volume  = {25},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {187--217},
  doi     = {10.1086/209535}
}
```
The definitive synthesis of the constructive-preferences view: consumers often build their preferences on the spot using context-dependent heuristics rather than retrieving stable, pre-formed values. The paper organizes decades of process-tracing evidence into a framework for how task, context, and effort-accuracy trade-offs shape choice. A cornerstone reference for behavioral decision research in marketing.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{luce1999emotional,
  author  = {Luce, Mary Frances and Payne, John W. and Bettman, James R.},
  title   = {Emotional Trade-Off Difficulty and Choice},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1999},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {143--159},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224379903600201}
}
```
Shows that decisions requiring emotionally difficult trade-offs (e.g., trading safety against price) lead consumers to adopt coping strategies that alter how they process information and choose. Negative emotion from trade-off difficulty changes decision processes, not just outcomes. A foundational study linking emotion to the mechanics of consumer choice.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{fitzsimons2008automatic,
  author  = {Fitzsimons, Gr\'ainne M. and Chartrand, Tanya L. and Fitzsimons, Gavan J.},
  title   = {Automatic Effects of Brand Exposure on Motivated Behavior: How Apple Makes You ``Think Different''},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {21--35},
  doi     = {10.1086/527269}
}
```
Provides experimental evidence that subliminal exposure to a brand logo (Apple vs. IBM) can nonconsciously prime brand-congruent behavior, with Apple-primed participants behaving more creatively. The study is a flagship demonstration of nonconscious brand priming and the behavioral reach of brand associations. A frequently cited result on automatic brand effects.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{ariely2003coherent,
  author  = {Ariely, Dan and Loewenstein, George and Prelec, Drazen},
  title   = {``Coherent Arbitrariness'': Stable Demand Curves Without Stable Preferences},
  journal = {The Quarterly Journal of Economics},
  year    = {2003},
  volume  = {118},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {73--106},
  doi     = {10.1162/00335530360535153}
}
```
Shows that consumers' absolute willingness to pay can be anchored to arbitrary numbers, yet their relative valuations remain orderly — "coherent arbitrariness." The finding undermines the assumption of stable, pre-existing preferences while preserving demand-curve structure, with deep implications for pricing. A seminal behavioral-economics result on the construction of value.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{moorman1999role,
  author  = {Moorman, Christine and Rust, Roland T.},
  title   = {The Role of Marketing},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {1999},
  volume  = {63},
  number  = {4_suppl1},
  pages   = {180--197},
  doi     = {10.1177/00222429990634s117}
}
```
A field-defining essay on what the marketing function contributes to the firm, arguing that marketing's value lies in connecting the customer to product, service-delivery, and financial-accountability processes. The paper helped reframe marketing as a cross-functional, value-delivering capability rather than a department. A touchstone for the marketing-strategy and marketing-accountability literatures.
Chapter: marketing-strategy

```bibtex
@article{swaminathan2009alliances,
  author  = {Swaminathan, Vanitha and Moorman, Christine},
  title   = {Marketing Alliances, Firm Networks, and Firm Value Creation},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {73},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {52--69},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.73.5.52}
}
```
Examines how a firm's position in marketing-alliance networks affects the value created when it forms new alliances, using event-study methods to link network structure to shareholder value. The authors show that network centrality, density, and prior experience condition the market's reaction. A key study connecting marketing strategy to firm financial performance.
Chapter: metrics

## Harvard Business School

```bibtex
@article{gourville1998pennies,
  author  = {Gourville, John T.},
  title   = {Pennies-a-Day: The Effect of Temporal Reframing on Transaction Evaluation},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {1998},
  volume  = {24},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {395--403},
  doi     = {10.1086/209517}
}
```
Demonstrates that reframing a large aggregate cost into a series of small ongoing payments ("pennies a day") makes an expense feel more affordable and increases compliance, because consumers compare it to trivial daily expenditures. The temporal-reframing mechanism has direct applications to pricing and fundraising appeals. A classic result on how payment framing shapes transaction evaluation.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{gupta2004valuing,
  author  = {Gupta, Sunil and Lehmann, Donald R. and Stuart, Jennifer Ames},
  title   = {Valuing Customers},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {41},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {7--18},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.41.1.7.25084}
}
```
Provides a simple, widely adopted method for estimating customer lifetime value and aggregating it to value a firm's entire customer base, then validates the approach against the market capitalizations of real companies. The paper made "customer equity as firm value" an empirically tractable idea for managers and investors. A foundational reference for CLV-based firm valuation.
Chapter: clv

```bibtex
@article{norton2012ikea,
  author  = {Norton, Michael I. and Mochon, Daniel and Ariely, Dan},
  title   = {The {IKEA} Effect: When Labor Leads to Love},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Psychology},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {22},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {453--460},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002}
}
```
Coins and tests the "IKEA effect": consumers place a disproportionately high value on products they partially built themselves, even when the results are amateurish. The labor-leads-to-love mechanism reshapes how firms think about co-creation, customization, and effort in product design. A widely cited demonstration of how self-investment inflates valuation.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{mochon2012competence,
  author  = {Mochon, Daniel and Norton, Michael I. and Ariely, Dan},
  title   = {Bolstering and Restoring Feelings of Competence via the {IKEA} Effect},
  journal = {International Journal of Research in Marketing},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {29},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {363--369},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.ijresmar.2012.05.001}
}
```
Extends the IKEA effect by showing that successfully completing a self-assembly task bolsters consumers' feelings of competence, and that consumers seek out such tasks to restore threatened competence. The paper identifies a self-signaling mechanism behind the value of co-creation. A useful companion that explains why labor leads to love.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{elberse2007stars,
  author  = {Elberse, Anita},
  title   = {The Power of Stars: Do Star Actors Drive the Success of Movies?},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2007},
  volume  = {71},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {102--120},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.71.4.102}
}
```
Uses an event-study of casting announcements to test whether star actors create value for the films and studios that hire them. Elberse finds that stars can raise revenue expectations but that their effect on studio shareholder value is far more limited, complicating the "stars at any price" logic. A rigorous empirical anchor for the economics of entertainment and superstars.
Chapter: marketing-strategy

```bibtex
@article{elberse2010bundles,
  author  = {Elberse, Anita},
  title   = {Bye-Bye Bundles: The Unbundling of Music in Digital Channels},

```
Examines what happens to revenue when digital distribution lets consumers buy individual songs instead of full albums, quantifying the demand and revenue consequences of unbundling. Elberse shows that à la carte purchasing can erode revenue for catalog owners, with broad implications for digital-goods pricing. A leading empirical study of unbundling and digital-content strategy.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{eliashberg2006motionpicture,
  author  = {Eliashberg, Jehoshua and Elberse, Anita and Leenders, Mark A. A. M.},

A comprehensive review of marketing research on the motion-picture industry, organizing the field around production, distribution, and exhibition decisions and identifying open research questions. It remains the standard map of entertainment-marketing research and its managerial stakes. A valuable orienting reference for the entertainment and innovation literatures.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{teixeira2012emotion,
  author  = {Teixeira, Thales and Wedel, Michel and Pieters, Rik},

Teixeira and co-authors link specific emotional dynamics to concrete advertising-effectiveness outcomes. A frontier study of attention and emotion in digital video advertising.
Chapter: advertising
