# Washington University in St. Louis (Olin Business School) — Marketing Scholarship

Verified bibliography of influential and frontier marketing scholarship associated with WashU Olin faculty. Every entry below has a DOI confirmed against Crossref (version-of-record, journal-article DOIs — no SSRN/preprints). Items that could not be verified are omitted rather than guessed.

Verification protocol: each paper located via `https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=...` and metadata (title, authors, year, journal, volume/issue/pages) confirmed against `https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`.

## Structural Models

### Seetharaman — Multiple sources of state dependence

```bibtex
@article{seetharaman2004statedependence,
  author  = {Seetharaman, P. B.},
  title   = {Modeling Multiple Sources of State Dependence in Random Utility Models: A Distributed Lag Approach},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {23},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {263--271},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1030.0024}
}
```

Develops a distributed-lag random-utility framework that disentangles distinct sources of structural state dependence (e.g., habit persistence versus purchase-event feedback) in choice data. A methodological touchstone for empirically separating true state dependence from heterogeneity in panel choice models.

Chapter: structural-models

### Chan, Narasimhan & Zhang — Dynamic structural model of flexible consumption

```bibtex
@article{chan2008flexibleconsumption,
  author  = {Chan, Tat and Narasimhan, Chakravarthi and Zhang, Qin},
  title   = {Decomposing Promotional Effects with a Dynamic Structural Model of Flexible Consumption},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {45},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {487--498},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.45.4.487}
}
```

Builds a dynamic structural model in which consumers' consumption rate is endogenous, allowing promotional response to be decomposed into stockpiling versus incremental consumption. Shows that ignoring flexible consumption biases estimates of promotion profitability.

Chapter: structural-models

### Cosguner, Chan & Seetharaman — Dynamic channel pricing with switching costs

```bibtex
@article{cosguner2018switchingcosts,
  author  = {Cosguner, Koray and Chan, Tat Y. and Seetharaman, P. B.},
  title   = {Dynamic Pricing in a Distribution Channel in the Presence of Switching Costs},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {64},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {1212--1229},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2016.2649}
}
```

Estimates a structural model of manufacturer-retailer pricing when consumer inertia (switching costs) creates inter-temporal demand linkages. Finds the retailer exploits inertial demand by raising retail margins, with implications for channel coordination and dynamic pass-through.

Chapter: structural-models

## Consumer Behavior

### Seetharaman, Ainslie & Chintagunta — State dependence across categories

```bibtex
@article{seetharaman1999crosscategory,
  author  = {Seetharaman, P. B. and Ainslie, Andrew and Chintagunta, Pradeep K.},
  title   = {Investigating Household State Dependence Effects across Categories},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1999},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {488--500},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224379903600407}
}
```

Estimates household-level brand-choice state dependence jointly across multiple product categories, allowing the strength of inertia/variety-seeking to be compared. Demonstrates systematic, category-specific patterns in how past purchases shape current choice.

Chapter: consumer-behavior

### LeBoeuf & Norton — Consequence-cause matching

```bibtex
@article{leboeuf2012consequencecause,
  author  = {LeBoeuf, Robyn A. and Norton, Michael I.},
  title   = {Consequence-Cause Matching: Looking to the Consequences of Events to Infer Their Causes},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {39},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {128--141},
  doi     = {10.1086/662372}
}
```

Documents a judgment bias whereby people infer that large consequences must have large (or matching) causes, distorting causal attribution in consumption and risk settings. A clean demonstration of a heuristic shaping consumer inference.

Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Nudges

### Rick, Cryder & Loewenstein — Tightwads and spendthrifts

```bibtex
@article{rick2008tightwads,
  author  = {Rick, Scott I. and Cryder, Cynthia E. and Loewenstein, George},
  title   = {Tightwads and Spendthrifts},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {34},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {767--782},
  doi     = {10.1086/523285}
}
```

Introduces the "pain of paying" individual-difference construct and the Spendthrift-Tightwad scale, showing that chronic spending reluctance is driven by anticipatory pain rather than by attitudes or liquidity. Foundational for behavioral pricing and payment-design nudges.

Chapter: nudges

### Cryder, Lerner, Gross & Dahl — Misery is not miserly

```bibtex
@article{cryder2008misery,
  author  = {Cryder, Cynthia E. and Lerner, Jennifer S. and Gross, James J. and Dahl, Ronald E.},
  title   = {Misery Is Not Miserly: Sad and Self-Focused Individuals Spend More},
  journal = {Psychological Science},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {19},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {525--530},
  doi     = {10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02118.x}
}
```

Shows experimentally that induced sadness combined with self-focus increases willingness to pay, linking incidental emotion to spending behavior. Influential evidence on how affective states are carried over into financial decisions.

Chapter: nudges

### Gershon & Cryder — Goods donations and charitable credit

```bibtex
@article{gershon2018goodsdonations,
  author  = {Gershon, Rachel and Cryder, Cynthia},
  title   = {Goods Donations Increase Charitable Credit for Low-Warmth Donors},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {45},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {451--469},
  doi     = {10.1093/jcr/ucx126}
}
```

Finds that donating goods rather than money confers more interpersonal "charitable credit," especially for donors perceived as low in warmth, because goods signal effort and care. Relevant to charitable-appeal design and prosocial consumer behavior.

Chapter: nudges

## Pricing

### Narasimhan — Competitive promotional strategies

```bibtex
@article{narasimhan1988promotion,
  author  = {Narasimhan, Chakravarthi},
  title   = {Competitive Promotional Strategies},
  journal = {The Journal of Business},
  year    = {1988},
  volume  = {61},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {427--449},
  doi     = {10.1086/296442}
}
```

A seminal analytical treatment of price promotions as a mixed-strategy equilibrium device for price-discriminating between loyal and switching consumers. Established a canonical rationale for why competing firms randomize promotional discounts.

Chapter: pricing

### Chen, Narasimhan & Zhang — Price-matching guarantees

```bibtex
@article{chen2001pricematching,
  author  = {Chen, Yuxin and Narasimhan, Chakravarthi and Zhang, Z. John},
  title   = {Consumer Heterogeneity and Competitive Price-Matching Guarantees},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2001},
  volume  = {20},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {300--314},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.20.3.300.9766}
}
```

Shows analytically that price-matching guarantees can serve as a price-discrimination mechanism exploiting consumer heterogeneity in search/information costs, rather than purely as a collusive device. Reframes a common retail practice in competitive terms.

Chapter: pricing

## Market Entry

### Narasimhan & Zhang — Market entry under heterogeneity

```bibtex
@article{narasimhan2000marketentry,
  author  = {Narasimhan, Chakravarthi and Zhang, Z. John},
  title   = {Market Entry Strategy Under Firm Heterogeneity and Asymmetric Payoffs},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2000},
  volume  = {19},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {313--327},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.19.4.313.11790}
}
```

Analyzes entry and accommodation decisions when firms differ in capabilities and face asymmetric payoffs, characterizing when incumbents deter versus accommodate entry. A core analytical reference for entry strategy under competitive heterogeneity.

Chapter: market-entry

### Thomadsen — Location and competition in fast food

```bibtex
@article{thomadsen2007positioning,
  author  = {Thomadsen, Raphael},
  title   = {Product Positioning and Competition: The Role of Location in the Fast Food Industry},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2007},
  volume  = {26},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {792--804},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1070.0296}
}
```

Estimates a structural model of spatial demand and price competition in fast food, showing that firms may prefer to avoid close geographic competition when markets are large. Connects positioning/location choices to equilibrium pricing and profitability.

Chapter: market-entry

### Pazgal, Soberman & Thomadsen — Profit-increasing asymmetric entry

```bibtex
@article{pazgal2016asymmetricentry,
  author  = {Pazgal, Amit and Soberman, David and Thomadsen, Raphael},
  title   = {Profit-Increasing Asymmetric Entry},
  journal = {International Journal of Research in Marketing},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {33},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {107--122},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.ijresmar.2015.08.002}
}
```

Identifies conditions under which entry by an asymmetric competitor can raise industry — and even incumbent — profits by softening price competition through differentiation. A counterintuitive analytical result on the welfare and strategic effects of entry.

Chapter: market-entry

## Advertising

### Yao & Mela — Dynamic model of sponsored search advertising

```bibtex
@article{yao2011sponsoredsearch,
  author  = {Yao, Song and Mela, Carl F.},
  title   = {A Dynamic Model of Sponsored Search Advertising},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2011},
  volume  = {30},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {447--468},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1100.0626}
}
```

Develops a structural dynamic model of advertiser bidding and consumer clicking in keyword auctions, recovering advertiser valuations and quantifying auction-design effects on revenue. A frontier reference for the economics of search advertising.

Chapter: advertising

### Seiler & Yao — Advertising along the conversion funnel

```bibtex
@article{seiler2017conversionfunnel,
  author  = {Seiler, Stephan and Yao, Song},
  title   = {The Impact of Advertising along the Conversion Funnel},
  journal = {Quantitative Marketing and Economics},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {15},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {241--278},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11129-017-9184-y}
}
```

Uses detailed data to trace how advertising shifts consumers across stages of the purchase funnel (consideration through purchase), distinguishing where advertising has its largest marginal effect. Informs attribution and media-allocation across funnel stages.

Chapter: advertising

## Sales

### Chan, Li & Pierce — Learning from peers in the sales force

```bibtex
@article{chan2014peerlearning,
  author  = {Chan, Tat Y. and Li, Jia and Pierce, Lamar},
  title   = {Learning from Peers: Knowledge Transfer and Sales Force Productivity Growth},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {33},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {463--484},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2013.0831}
}
```

Provides field evidence that salespeople's productivity grows through peer knowledge transfer, and quantifies how team composition and compensation shape such spillovers. Bridges personnel economics and sales-force management.

Chapter: sales

### Yang, Chan & Thomadsen — Salesforce-driven model of consumer choice

```bibtex
@article{yang2019salesforcechoice,
  author  = {Yang, Bicheng and Chan, Tat and Thomadsen, Raphael},
  title   = {A Salesforce-Driven Model of Consumer Choice},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2019},
  volume  = {38},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {871--887},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2019.1175}
}
```

Models how individual salesperson effort and persuasion enter consumers' choices, integrating sales-force behavior into a structural demand framework. Shows how to attribute sales outcomes between product preferences and salesperson influence.

Chapter: sales

## Empirical Models

### Borzekowski, Thomadsen & Taragin — Competition and price discrimination

```bibtex
@article{borzekowski2009mailinglists,
  author  = {Borzekowski, Ron and Thomadsen, Raphael and Taragin, Charles},
  title   = {Competition and Price Discrimination in the Market for Mailing Lists},
  journal = {Quantitative Marketing and Economics},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {7},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {147--179},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11129-009-9050-7}
}
```

Empirically examines how competitive intensity affects the use of second- and third-degree price discrimination in the market for mailing lists. Provides evidence on the interaction between competition and price-discrimination strategy in a B2B information market.

Chapter: empirical-models
