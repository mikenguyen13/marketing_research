# USC Marshall (Marketing) — Verified Bibliography

Compiled for the marketing research book. Subject: influential and frontier marketing scholarship associated with the **University of Southern California, Marshall School of Business** (Department of Marketing). Several listed works predate an author's USC appointment (e.g., Mayzlin's WOM papers from her Yale years; Schwarz's foundational social-cognition work); they are included because the scholar is now a USC Marshall faculty member and these are their canonical contributions.

**Scope / exclusions (per editorial brief):** Gerard Tellis and Lan Luo are covered elsewhere and are excluded here. Also excluded as already gathered: Marinova, the conjoint set, the branding-frontier and advertising sets, and Davide Proserpio's `proserpio2017` (the JMR Airbnb-vs-hotels paper, DOI 10.1509/jmr.15.0204). Only **other** Proserpio works are listed below.

**Verification protocol:** Every item was checked against the Crossref REST API (`https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`). Title, author set, journal, year, and (where available) volume/issue/pages match the Crossref version-of-record metadata. Published version-of-record DOIs are used throughout (10.1509/, 10.1177/, 10.1287/, 10.1086/, 10.1093/, 10.1257/, 10.1207/, 10.1037/); no SSRN/preprint/NBER working-paper DOIs are cited even where they exist for the same title. Verified 2026-06-21.

Disambiguation note: Crossref returns many unrelated authors named "Sha Yang," "Sha," "Nunes," and "Schwarz" (engineering, public-health, and CS records). All such records were excluded; only works by the named USC Marshall marketing scholars (confirmed via coauthors and journals — e.g., Allenby, Ghose, Drèze, Zauberman, Zervas) are listed.

## Word of Mouth & Online Reviews (Dina Mayzlin)

```bibtex
@article{chevalier2006wom,
  author  = {Chevalier, Judith A. and Mayzlin, Dina},
  title   = {The Effect of Word of Mouth on Sales: Online Book Reviews},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2006},
  volume  = {43},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {345--354},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.43.3.345}
}
```
A foundational empirical study linking online consumer reviews to sales, using a natural experiment across Amazon and Barnes & Noble to show that an improvement in a book's reviews raises its relative sales. It also documents that consumers read review text, not just star averages (1-star reviews matter more than 5-star ones). One of the most-cited early demonstrations that user-generated reviews move the market.
Chapter: virality

```bibtex
@article{mayzlin2006chat,
  author  = {Mayzlin, Dina},
  title   = {Promotional Chat on the Internet},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2006},
  volume  = {25},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {155--163},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1050.0137}
}
```
An analytical model of online word of mouth in which firms can post anonymous promotional messages disguised as organic consumer chat. Mayzlin shows that in equilibrium such manipulation is partially undone by rational consumers, yet firms still find it profitable to engage in it, and the lower-quality firm advertises more aggressively through fake chat. A seminal theory companion to the empirical review-manipulation literature.
Chapter: analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{mayzlin2014promotional,
  author  = {Mayzlin, Dina and Dover, Yaniv and Chevalier, Judith},
  title   = {Promotional Reviews: An Empirical Investigation of Online Review Manipulation},
  journal = {American Economic Review},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {104},
  number  = {8},
  pages   = {2421--2455},
  doi     = {10.1257/aer.104.8.2421}
}
```
This study identifies review manipulation by comparing the same hotels' reviews across TripAdvisor (anyone can post) and Expedia (only verified guests). Hotels with the most to gain (independent, single-unit, neighbors of competitors) have a disproportionate share of suspicious five-star and one-star reviews. A landmark empirical detection of strategic fake reviews and the platform features that deter them.
Chapter: virality

```bibtex
@article{pei2022affiliation,
  author  = {Pei, Amy and Mayzlin, Dina},
  title   = {Influencing Social Media Influencers Through Affiliation},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2022},
  volume  = {41},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {593--615},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2021.1322}
}
```
A game-theoretic analysis of affiliate compensation for social-media influencers, where an influencer's recommendation carries both informational and commercial weight. The authors show how affiliate links distort recommendations and characterize when affiliation helps versus hurts the firm and consumers. A frontier theory contribution bridging WOM and the influencer-marketing economy.
Chapter: communication

## Channels, Retailing & Media Economics (Anthony Dukes)

```bibtex
@article{dukes2010instore,
  author  = {Dukes, Anthony and Liu, Yunchuan},
  title   = {In-Store Media and Distribution Channel Coordination},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2010},
  volume  = {29},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {94--107},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1080.0483}
}
```
An analytical model of in-store advertising media (e.g., shopping-cart and shelf ads) as a tool that can coordinate the manufacturer–retailer channel. The authors show in-store media revenue alters the retailer's incentives and can mitigate double-marginalization, with implications for who controls the retail media network. An early theory of what is now the booming "retail media" category.
Chapter: analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{zhu2015selective,
  author  = {Zhu, Yi and Dukes, Anthony},
  title   = {Selective Reporting of Factual Content by Commercial Media},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {52},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {56--76},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.12.0379}
}
```
A model of how ad-supported commercial media strategically select which factual content to report to attract audiences and maximize advertising revenue. The authors show selective (not false) reporting arises endogenously and characterize when more competition among media improves or degrades information quality. A theoretical contribution at the marketing–media-economics interface.
Chapter: analytical-models

```bibtex
@article{zhu2017prominent,
  author  = {Zhu, Yi and Dukes, Anthony},
  title   = {Prominent Attributes Under Limited Attention},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {683--698},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2017.1037}
}
```
This analytical paper studies how firms make certain product attributes "prominent" to exploit consumers' limited attention, and how competition shapes which attributes get highlighted. The authors derive conditions under which attribute prominence intensifies or softens price competition. A frontier behavioral-IO theory of attention and product presentation.
Chapter: analytical-models

## Bayesian Modeling, Choice & Search Advertising (Sha Yang)

```bibtex
@article{yang2003interdependent,
  author  = {Yang, Sha and Allenby, Greg M.},
  title   = {Modeling Interdependent Consumer Preferences},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2003},
  volume  = {40},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {282--294},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.40.3.282.19240}
}
```
A Bayesian spatial-autoregressive model of consumer preferences in which an individual's utility depends on the preferences of others in a defined network or geography. Yang and Allenby show how to estimate such interdependence and apply it to automobile-purchase data, capturing social and spatial correlation. A methodological cornerstone for modeling preference interdependence with hierarchical Bayes.
Chapter: empirical-models

```bibtex
@article{yang2010organic,
  author  = {Yang, Sha and Ghose, Anindya},
  title   = {Analyzing the Relationship Between Organic and Sponsored Search Advertising: Positive, Negative, or Zero Interdependence?},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2010},
  volume  = {29},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {602--623},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1090.0552}
}
```
Using a simultaneous-equations Bayesian model on paid-search data, the authors estimate the interdependence between organic and paid (sponsored) search listings, finding a positive synergy: presence in organic results raises the returns to paid advertising and vice versa. A widely cited empirical resolution of a then-open question in search-engine marketing.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{lu2017keyword,
  author  = {Lu, Shijie and Yang, Sha},
  title   = {Investigating the Spillover Effect of Keyword Market Entry in Sponsored Search Advertising},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {976--998},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2017.1053}
}
```
This empirical study examines cross-keyword spillovers when an advertiser enters a new keyword market in sponsored search, showing that entry on one keyword affects performance on related keywords. The authors quantify these spillovers and their managerial implications for keyword portfolio decisions. A frontier contribution on the dynamics of search-advertising portfolios.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{yang2022platform,
  author  = {Yang, Botao and Yang, Sha and Dutta, Shantanu},
  title   = {Platform Service Offering to Business Customers: Strategic Considerations in Engendering Seller Use of Marketing Tools},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2022},
  volume  = {41},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {361--379},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2021.1325}
}
```
This paper studies how a platform should offer marketing tools and services to its third-party sellers (business customers) to encourage adoption and grow platform value. It combines strategic modeling with empirical analysis of seller behavior on a marketplace platform. A recent contribution on platform-side marketing in two-sided marketplaces.
Chapter: empirical-models

## Status, Luxury & Loyalty Structure (Joseph Nunes)

```bibtex
@article{nunes2006endowed,
  author  = {Nunes, Joseph C. and Dr{\`e}ze, Xavier},
  title   = {The Endowed Progress Effect: How Artificial Advancement Increases Effort},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2006},
  volume  = {32},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {504--512},
  doi     = {10.1086/500480}
}
```
A classic behavioral finding: giving consumers an artificial head start toward a goal (e.g., a loyalty card pre-stamped with "bonus" purchases) increases the rate at which they complete it, relative to an equivalent shorter program. The "endowed progress effect" is now a staple of loyalty-program and gamification design. Highly cited in both consumer behavior and applied marketing.
Chapter: nudges

```bibtex
@article{dreze2009superior,
  author  = {Dr{\`e}ze, Xavier and Nunes, Joseph C.},
  title   = {Feeling Superior: The Impact of Loyalty Program Structure on Consumers' Perceptions of Status},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {890--905},
  doi     = {10.1086/593946}
}
```
This paper shows that the perceived value of an elite loyalty tier depends on its relative structure — how many others are above and below the consumer — not just absolute benefits. Status is felt most when a meaningful group sits below you, so tier design can manufacture or erode the feeling of superiority. A foundational reference for hierarchical loyalty-program and status design.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{han2010signaling,
  author  = {Han, Young Jee and Nunes, Joseph C. and Dr{\`e}ze, Xavier},
  title   = {Signaling Status with Luxury Goods: The Role of Brand Prominence},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2010},
  volume  = {74},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {15--30},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.74.4.015}
}
```
This influential paper introduces the "loud vs. quiet" luxury taxonomy: consumers' wealth and need-for-status jointly determine whether they prefer conspicuously branded ("loud") or subtly branded ("quiet") luxury goods. It maps four signaling segments (Patricians, Parvenus, Poseurs, Proletarians) and shows brand prominence is a deliberate signaling lever. A canonical reference on luxury branding and conspicuous consumption.
Chapter: branding

## Consumer Experience, Choice & Photo-Taking (Kristin Diehl)

```bibtex
@article{lamberton2013retail,
  author  = {Lamberton, Cait Poynor and Diehl, Kristin},
  title   = {Retail Choice Architecture: The Effects of Benefit- and Attribute-Based Assortment Organization on Consumer Perceptions and Choice},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2013},
  volume  = {40},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {393--411},
  doi     = {10.1086/671103}
}
```
This paper shows that organizing a retail assortment by *benefits* (vs. by *attributes*) changes how consumers perceive variety, their satisfaction, and what they choose — often increasing perceived variety and willingness to explore. It positions assortment organization as a choice-architecture lever distinct from the assortment's actual content. A key reference linking retailing, choice architecture, and consumer perception.
Chapter: nudges

```bibtex
@article{diehl2016photos,
  author  = {Diehl, Kristin and Zauberman, Gal and Barasch, Alixandra},
  title   = {How Taking Photos Increases Enjoyment of Experiences},
  journal = {Journal of Personality and Social Psychology},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {111},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {119--140}
}
```
Across nine experiments (field and lab), the authors show that taking photos during an experience generally increases enjoyment by heightening engagement and attention, contrary to the common worry that photography distracts. They also map boundary conditions where photo-taking does not help. A widely cited program piece on the psychology of experiential consumption. (DOI: 10.1037/pspa0000055)
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{chun2017savoring,
  author  = {Chun, HaeEun Helen and Diehl, Kristin and MacInnis, Deborah J.},
  title   = {Savoring an Upcoming Experience Affects Ongoing and Remembered Consumption Enjoyment},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {81},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {96--110},
  doi     = {10.1509/jm.15.0267}
}
```
This paper demonstrates that anticipatory savoring of an upcoming experience raises both in-the-moment and remembered enjoyment of the current consumption episode. The authors trace the mechanism and its implications for how marketers stage and sequence experiences. A consumer-experience contribution relevant to services and experiential marketing.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Metacognition & Judgment (Norbert Schwarz)

```bibtex
@article{schwarz1991ease,
  author  = {Schwarz, Norbert and Bless, Herbert and Strack, Fritz and Klumpp, Gisela and Rittenauer-Schatka, Helga and Simons, Annette},
  title   = {Ease of Retrieval as Information: Another Look at the Availability Heuristic},
  journal = {Journal of Personality and Social Psychology},
  year    = {1991},
  volume  = {61},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {195--202},
  doi     = {10.1037/0022-3514.61.2.195}
}
```
The seminal demonstration that judgments rely on the *subjective ease* of recalling examples, not just the number recalled: people asked to list 12 assertive behaviors rate themselves as *less* assertive than those asked for 6, because 12 feels hard to generate. This "ease-of-retrieval" effect is foundational to metacognition research and pervasive in consumer judgment. One of the most influential papers in social cognition.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{schwarz2004metacognitive,
  author  = {Schwarz, Norbert},
  title   = {Metacognitive Experiences in Consumer Judgment and Decision Making},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Psychology},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {14},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {332--348},
  doi     = {10.1207/s15327663jcp1404_2}
}
```
Schwarz's integrative statement of how metacognitive experiences — fluency, ease of retrieval, and the feelings that accompany thinking — inform consumer judgments independently of the declarative content recalled. It organizes a large literature into a framework directly applicable to advertising, branding, and choice. A standard reference for metacognition in consumer behavior.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Consumer Finance & Spending (Stephanie Tully)

```bibtex
@article{tully2015material,
  author  = {Tully, Stephanie M. and Hershfield, Hal E. and Meyvis, Tom},
  title   = {Seeking Lasting Enjoyment with Limited Money: Financial Constraints Increase Preference for Material Goods over Experiences},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {42},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {59--75},
  doi     = {10.1093/jcr/ucv007}
}
```
This paper qualifies the well-known "buy experiences, not things" prescription by showing that financially constrained consumers prefer *material* goods, because material possessions are perceived to provide more lasting value per dollar. It identifies a financial-constraint mechanism (long-term focus) that reverses the usual experiential-advantage finding. An important consumer-finance and well-being contribution.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{tully2018debt,
  author  = {Tully, Stephanie M. and Sharma, Eesha},
  title   = {Context-Dependent Drivers of Discretionary Debt Decisions: Explaining Willingness to Borrow for Experiential Purchases},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {44},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {960--973},
  doi     = {10.1093/jcr/ucx078}
}
```
The authors study when consumers are willing to take on discretionary debt, showing that willingness to borrow for experiences versus material goods depends systematically on decision context (e.g., the salience of the purchase's longevity). The work connects purchase type to consumer borrowing and financial decision-making. A core consumer-finance behavioral paper.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{delarosa2022payment,
  author  = {De La Rosa, Wendy and Tully, Stephanie M.},
  title   = {The Impact of Payment Frequency on Consumer Spending and Subjective Wealth Perceptions},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2022},
  volume  = {48},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {991--1009},
  doi     = {10.1093/jcr/ucab052}
}
```
This research shows that receiving income more frequently (e.g., weekly vs. monthly) raises subjective perceptions of wealth and, in turn, increases spending, even holding total income constant. It has direct implications for payroll design, budgeting tools, and financial well-being. A recent, policy-relevant consumer-finance contribution.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Platforms & Online Reviews (Davide Proserpio — works other than proserpio2017)

```bibtex
@article{proserpio2017reputation,
  author  = {Proserpio, Davide and Zervas, Georgios},
  title   = {Online Reputation Management: Estimating the Impact of Management Responses on Consumer Reviews},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {36},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {645--665},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2017.1043}
}
```
Using hotel-review panels from TripAdvisor and Expedia, the authors show that when hotels begin publicly responding to reviews, their ratings improve — largely because dissatisfied customers become less likely to leave negative reviews when management is seen responding. A foundational empirical study of management responses as a reputation-management lever. (Distinct from the excluded `proserpio2017` JMR Airbnb-vs-hotels paper; this is the Marketing Science management-responses paper.)
Chapter: virality

```bibtex
@article{zervas2021reputation,
  author  = {Zervas, Georgios and Proserpio, Davide and Byers, John W.},
  title   = {A First Look at Online Reputation on Airbnb, Where Every Stay Is Above Average},
  journal = {Marketing Letters},
  year    = {2021},
  volume  = {32},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {1--16},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11002-020-09546-4}
}
```
This study documents extreme positive skew in Airbnb ratings (the vast majority of listings rate 4.5–5 stars) and compares Airbnb's reputation system to TripAdvisor's, arguing that platform design — two-sided reviews and social pressure — inflates ratings. A key descriptive contribution on how platform mechanics shape review distributions. (Published online 2020; print 2021.)
Chapter: virality

```bibtex
@article{barron2021homesharing,
  author  = {Barron, Kyle and Kung, Edward and Proserpio, Davide},
  title   = {The Effect of Home-Sharing on House Prices and Rents: Evidence from Airbnb},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2021},
  volume  = {40},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {23--47},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2020.1227}
}
```
Using national listing and housing data with a shift-share instrument, the authors estimate that Airbnb activity raises both house prices and rents, with the rent effect driven by units shifting from long-term to short-term rental. A widely cited empirical study on the spillover effects of platform-mediated sharing-economy activity. A frontier paper on platform externalities.
Chapter: empirical-models

## Notes on Scope & Exclusions

- **SSRN / NBER working-paper DOIs excluded** even where they share a title with a cited published paper. Examples encountered and *not* cited: Chevalier & Mayzlin NBER w10148; Mayzlin–Dover–Chevalier NBER w18340 and SSRN 2128860 (cited instead as AER 10.1257/aer.104.8.2421); Tully & Sharma SSRN 2738604; De La Rosa & Tully SSRN 3658727; numerous Proserpio SSRN preprints. The version-of-record DOI is used in every case.
- **Excluded per brief (covered elsewhere):** Tellis, Lan Luo, Marinova, the conjoint set, branding-frontier and advertising sets, and Proserpio's `proserpio2017` (JMR Airbnb-vs-hotels, 10.1509/jmr.15.0204 — verified to exist but deliberately omitted here).
- **Godes & Mayzlin WOM papers** ("Using Online Conversations to Study Word-of-Mouth," Mktg Sci 2004, 10.1287/mksc.1040.0071; "Firm-Created Word-of-Mouth," Mktg Sci 2009, 10.1287/mksc.1080.0444) are verified and available if additional WOM coverage is wanted, but the lead author (Godes) is not USC Marshall, so they are listed here only as a note rather than as primary USC items.
- **Schwarz / Diehl pre-USC and psychology-journal works:** Schwarz's 1991 JPSP and Diehl's JPSP paper are non-marketing-journal outlets but are the canonical version-of-record publications of these scholars' most-cited findings; included deliberately for the consumer-behavior chapters.

None. Every item in the themed sections above was confirmed against Crossref metadata (title, authors, journal, year, and where available volume/issue/pages). No DOI was inferred or constructed, and no SSRN/preprint DOI was substituted for a version of record.
