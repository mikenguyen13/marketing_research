# UC San Diego (Rady School of Management) — Verified Marketing Bibliography

Influential and frontier marketing scholarship associated with UC San Diego's Rady School of Management (plus closely affiliated UCSD economists working on giving/behavior). Every entry below has a DOI verified against Crossref. All are published version-of-record DOIs (no SSRN/preprint/dataset DOIs are cited). Annotations and chapter tags map each work to the book's chapters.

Verification method: queried `https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=...` and confirmed metadata (title/authors/journal/year/DOI) against `https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`. Faculty already gathered elsewhere (Tellis, Marinova, the conjoint set, the branding-frontier and advertising sets) are not re-listed.

Notes on affiliations: Crossref records the affiliation at time of publication, so some entries list authors' prior institutions (e.g., Karmarkar's price-primacy paper lists HBS). The scholars are current or former Rady marketing faculty, or UCSD economists (Andreoni) whose giving research is foundational to Rady's behavioral/charitable-giving cluster.

## Advertising

```bibtex
@article{wilbur2008twosided,
  author  = {Wilbur, Kenneth C.},
  title   = {A Two-Sided, Empirical Model of Television Advertising and Viewing Markets},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {27},
  number  = {3},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1070.0303}
}
```
Wilbur builds a two-sided structural model in which advertisers and viewers jointly determine TV market outcomes, estimating how ad quantity affects viewership and vice versa. The framework quantifies the externality networks impose when they sell more ad time, and is a benchmark for media-market modeling. A foundational Rady advertising paper bridging empirical IO and marketing.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{wilbur2008dvr,
  author  = {Wilbur, Kenneth C.},
  title   = {How the Digital Video Recorder (DVR) Changes Traditional Television Advertising: A Model of the Audience},
  journal = {Journal of Advertising},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {37},
  number  = {1},
  doi     = {10.2753/JOA0091-3367370111}
}
```
Wilbur models how DVR-enabled ad-skipping reshapes the value of traditional TV advertising and the incentives of networks and advertisers. It was an early, influential treatment of advertising avoidance as a strategic and measurement problem. Useful for the chapter's discussion of media disruption and exposure measurement.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{joo2014tvsearch,
  author  = {Joo, Mingyu and Wilbur, Kenneth C. and Cowgill, Bo and Zhu, Yi},
  title   = {Television Advertising and Online Search},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {60},
  number  = {1},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.2013.1741}
}
```
This paper shows that television advertising measurably increases consumers' online search activity for advertised brands, linking offline ad exposure to a digital behavioral trace. It is an early cross-media attribution study quantifying TV's spillover into search. Central to discussions of advertising effectiveness measurement across channels.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{joo2016tvkeyword,
  author  = {Joo, Mingyu and Wilbur, Kenneth C. and Zhu, Yi},
  title   = {Effects of TV Advertising on Keyword Search},
  journal = {International Journal of Research in Marketing},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {33},
  number  = {3},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.ijresmar.2014.12.005}
}
```
A companion analysis decomposing how TV advertising shifts the volume and composition of branded versus generic keyword search. It refines the cross-channel measurement question by separating navigational from exploratory search responses. Relevant to attribution and the interplay of paid media and search.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{wilbur2016avoidance,
  author  = {Wilbur, Kenneth C.},
  title   = {Advertising Content and Television Advertising Avoidance},
  journal = {Journal of Media Economics},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {29},
  number  = {2},
  doi     = {10.1080/08997764.2016.1170022}
}
```
Wilbur examines how the content of ads themselves affects whether viewers avoid them, moving beyond structural avoidance (e.g., DVRs) to creative determinants. The work connects advertising creative to measurable exposure loss. A bridge between the advertising and metrics chapters.
Chapter: advertising

## Pricing

```bibtex
@article{nijs2007retailprice,
  author  = {Nijs, Vincent R. and Srinivasan, Shuba and Pauwels, Koen},
  title   = {Retail-Price Drivers and Retailer Profits},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2007},
  volume  = {26},
  number  = {4},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1060.0205}
}
```
Using persistence modeling on store-level data, the authors decompose what drives retail prices over time (competition, costs, demand, past prices) and link those drivers to retailer profitability. It is a widely cited empirical pricing study with a time-series identification strategy. Strong fit for empirical pricing dynamics.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{srinivasan2008demandprice,
  author  = {Srinivasan, Shuba and Pauwels, Koen and Nijs, Vincent},
  title   = {Demand-Based Pricing versus Past-Price Dependence: A Cost-Benefit Analysis},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {72},
  number  = {2},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.72.2.15}
}
```
The paper contrasts forward-looking demand-based pricing with inertial past-price dependence, quantifying the profit cost of pricing on history rather than demand. It offers managers a framework for evaluating pricing rules. Bridges empirical pricing and managerial decision rules.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{nijs2014categorycaptain,
  author  = {Nijs, Vincent R. and Misra, Kanishka and Hansen, Karsten},
  title   = {Outsourcing Retail Pricing to a Category Captain: The Role of Information Firewalls},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {33},
  number  = {1},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2013.0822}
}
```
This study analyzes when a retailer should delegate category pricing to a manufacturer "category captain" and how information firewalls mitigate the captain's conflict of interest. It blends analytical modeling with channel-pricing institutions. Relevant to vertical pricing strategy and channel design.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{handel2015robustpricing,
  author  = {Handel, Benjamin R. and Misra, Kanishka},
  title   = {Robust New Product Pricing},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {34},
  number  = {6},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2015.0914}
}
```
The authors develop pricing rules for new products under deep demand uncertainty, deriving robust (minimax-regret style) prices when the firm cannot reliably estimate demand. It is an analytical contribution to pricing under ambiguity. Fits the analytical-models and pricing intersection.
Chapter: pricing

```bibtex
@article{misra2019banditpricing,
  author  = {Misra, Kanishka and Schwartz, Eric M. and Abernethy, Jacob},
  title   = {Dynamic Online Pricing with Incomplete Information Using Multiarmed Bandit Experiments},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2019},
  volume  = {38},
  number  = {2},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.2018.1129}
}
```
This frontier paper applies multi-armed bandit algorithms to set and learn prices online when demand is unknown, balancing exploration and exploitation. It connects machine-learning experimentation to dynamic pricing practice. A strong data/analytics-driven pricing reference.
Chapter: pricing

## Consumer Behavior

```bibtex
@article{amir2007decisionsbyrules,
  author  = {Amir, On and Ariely, Dan},
  title   = {Decisions by Rules: The Case of Unwillingness to Pay for Beneficial Delays},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2007},
  volume  = {44},
  number  = {1},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.44.1.142}
}
```
Amir and Ariely show that consumers often decide by applying simple rules rather than weighing costs and benefits, leading them to refuse beneficial delays. The work documents a systematic gap between stated preferences and willingness to act. Core consumer-behavior evidence on heuristic decision-making.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{liu2008interruption,
  author  = {Liu, Wendy},
  title   = {Focusing on Desirability: The Effect of Decision Interruption and Suspension on Preferences},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {4},
  doi     = {10.1086/592126}
}
```
Liu finds that interrupting or suspending a decision shifts consumers toward focusing on desirability over feasibility, altering their final preferences. It illuminates how the timing and process of deciding, not just the options, shape choice. A consumer-behavior result with implications for choice-architecture and timing.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{karmarkar2016neuroscience,
  author  = {Karmarkar, Uma R. and Yoon, Carolyn},
  title   = {Consumer Neuroscience: Advances in Understanding Consumer Psychology},
  journal = {Current Opinion in Psychology},
  year    = {2016},
  volume  = {10},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.copsyc.2016.01.010}
}
```
This review synthesizes how neural and physiological methods advance our understanding of consumer psychology, mapping what neuroscience adds beyond behavioral measures. It is a useful entry point to the consumer-neuroscience frontier. Anchors a forward-looking consumer-behavior discussion.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

```bibtex
@article{clithero2023neuralmethods,
  author  = {Clithero, John A. and Karmarkar, Uma R. and Nave, Gideon and Plassmann, Hilke},
  title   = {Reconsidering the Path for Neural and Physiological Methods in Consumer Psychology},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Psychology},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {33},
  number  = {3},
  doi     = {10.1002/jcpy.1397}
}
```
A recent agenda-setting piece arguing for how neuroscience methods should be integrated (and where they add value) in consumer research. It tempers hype with methodological guidance for the field. Frontier reference for the consumer-behavior and methods discussion.
Chapter: consumer-behavior

## Metrics

```bibtex
@article{karmarkar2015costconscious,
  author  = {Karmarkar, Uma R. and Shiv, Baba and Knutson, Brian},
  title   = {Cost Conscious? The Neural and Behavioral Impact of Price Primacy on Decision Making},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2015},
  volume  = {52},
  number  = {4},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.13.0488}
}
```
Combining fMRI and behavioral experiments, the authors show that presenting price before product (price primacy) changes how consumers evaluate purchases, shifting them toward cost-based judgments. It links a concrete design choice to neural valuation processes and downstream choice. Bridges consumer neuroscience and pricing-presentation metrics.

## Nudges

```bibtex
@article{gneezy2010sharedresponsibility,
  author  = {Gneezy, Ayelet and Gneezy, Uri and Nelson, Leif D. and Brown, Amber},
  title   = {Shared Social Responsibility: A Field Experiment in Pay-What-You-Want Pricing and Charitable Giving},
  journal = {Science},
  year    = {2010},
  volume  = {329},
  number  = {5989},
  doi     = {10.1126/science.1186744}
}
```
This large field experiment shows that combining pay-what-you-want pricing with a charitable component (shared social responsibility) raises both revenue and giving relative to standard pricing or pure PWYW. It is a landmark demonstration of how pricing structure nudges prosocial behavior. Central to the nudges and pricing-structure discussion.
Chapter: nudges

```bibtex
@article{gneezy2012payingtobenice,
  author  = {Gneezy, Ayelet and Imas, Alex and Brown, Amber and Nelson, Leif D. and Norton, Michael I.},
  title   = {Paying to Be Nice: Consistency and Costly Prosocial Behavior},
  journal = {Management Science},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {58},
  number  = {1},
  doi     = {10.1287/mnsc.1110.1437}
}
```
The authors show that an initial costly prosocial act makes people more likely to behave prosocially again, consistent with self-signaling and consistency motives. It informs how early commitments can be leveraged to sustain good behavior. A behavioral mechanism relevant to nudge design.
Chapter: nudges

```bibtex
@article{schwartz2021optingin,
  author  = {Schwartz, Daniel and Keenan, Elizabeth A. and Imas, Alex and Gneezy, Ayelet},
  title   = {Opting-In to Prosocial Incentives},
  journal = {Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes},
  year    = {2021},
  volume  = {163},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.obhdp.2019.01.003}
}
```
This paper studies prosocial incentives (rewards tied to charitable outcomes) and how letting people opt in changes their effectiveness. It refines when linking effort to giving motivates behavior. Directly relevant to designing prosocial nudges and incentive schemes.
Chapter: nudges

```bibtex
@article{liu2008timeask,
  author  = {Liu, Wendy and Aaker, Jennifer},
  title   = {The Happiness of Giving: The Time-Ask Effect},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {3},
  doi     = {10.1086/588699}
}
```
Liu and Aaker show that asking people first about giving time (rather than money) shifts them into an emotional, happiness-oriented mindset that increases both donations of time and money. It is a classic framing/sequence nudge in charitable solicitation. Fits nudges and consumer-behavior.
Chapter: nudges

## Communication

```bibtex
@article{andreoni1990warmglow,
  author  = {Andreoni, James},
  title   = {Impure Altruism and Donations to Public Goods: A Theory of Warm-Glow Giving},
  journal = {The Economic Journal},
  year    = {1990},
  volume  = {100},
  number  = {401},
  doi     = {10.2307/2234133}
}
```
Andreoni's seminal theory introduces "warm-glow" giving: people derive private utility from the act of donating, not only from the public good produced. It reshaped the economics of charity and underpins much marketing of prosocial appeals. Foundational for understanding why donation messaging works.
Chapter: communication

```bibtex
@article{andreoni2011powerofasking,
  author  = {Andreoni, James and Rao, Justin M.},
  title   = {The Power of Asking: How Communication Affects Selfishness, Empathy, and Altruism},
  journal = {Journal of Public Economics},
  year    = {2011},
  volume  = {95},
  number  = {7-8},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.jpubeco.2010.12.008}
}
```
Through experiments, the authors show that the direction and content of communication (asking versus being asked) strongly shape empathy and generosity. It isolates communication itself as a lever on prosocial behavior. Directly relevant to persuasion and charitable-appeal messaging.
Chapter: communication

```bibtex
@article{andreoni2017avoidingask,
  author  = {Andreoni, James and Rao, Justin M. and Trachtman, Hannah},
  title   = {Avoiding the Ask: A Field Experiment on Altruism, Empathy, and Charitable Giving},
  journal = {Journal of Political Economy},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {125},
  number  = {3},
  doi     = {10.1086/691703}
}
```
This field experiment (with Salvation Army solicitors) shows many people actively avoid being asked to give, revealing that solicitation imposes a real psychic cost. It complicates simple "ask more" prescriptions for fundraising. Important for the ethics and design of solicitation communication.
Chapter: communication

```bibtex
@article{gneezy2014promises,
  author  = {Gneezy, Ayelet and Epley, Nicholas},
  title   = {Worth Keeping but Not Exceeding: Asymmetric Consequences of Breaking versus Exceeding Promises},
  journal = {Social Psychological and Personality Science},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {5},
  number  = {7},
  doi     = {10.1177/1948550614533134}
}
```
Gneezy and Epley find that breaking a promise is punished far more than exceeding it is rewarded, an asymmetry with clear implications for brand promises and service commitments. It informs how firms should communicate and manage expectations. Relevant to communication, satisfaction, and promise-keeping.
Chapter: communication
