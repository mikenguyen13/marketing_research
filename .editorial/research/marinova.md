# Detelina Marinova — Verified Bibliography

Compiled for the marketing research book. Subject: **Detelina Marinova**, Robert J. Trulaske Sr. College of Business, University of Missouri (marketing). Co-Editor-in-Chief, *Journal of Marketing* (2023–).

**Verification protocol:** Every item below was checked against the Crossref REST API (`https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`). Title, author set, journal, year, and (where available) volume/issue/pages match the Crossref version-of-record metadata. Published version-of-record DOIs are used throughout (10.1509/, 10.1177/, 10.1007/, 10.1093/, etc.); no SSRN/preprint DOIs are cited. Verified on 2026-06-21.

Disambiguation note: Crossref returns several unrelated authors named "Detelina" (Tocheva, Dineva, Grozeva, Kamburova, Komsiiska, Ovcharova). All such records were excluded; only works by the University of Missouri marketing scholar are listed.

## Frontline Service & Employees

```bibtex
@article{ye2007strategic,
  author  = {Ye, Jun and Marinova, Detelina and Singh, Jagdip},
  title   = {Strategic Change Implementation and Performance Loss in the Front Lines},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2007},
  volume  = {71},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {156--171},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.71.4.156}
}
```
Using multilevel longitudinal data from retail/service units, the authors show that top-down strategic change implementation produces a transient but real performance loss concentrated at the customer-facing front lines. They model the magnitude and recovery trajectory of this loss and identify frontline managerial mechanisms that buffer it. A foundational empirical demonstration that strategy execution failures are localized in frontline units.
Chapter: service

```bibtex
@article{marinova2008frontline,
  author  = {Marinova, Detelina and Ye, Jun and Singh, Jagdip},
  title   = {Do Frontline Mechanisms Matter? Impact of Quality and Productivity Orientations on Unit Revenue, Efficiency, and Customer Satisfaction},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {72},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {28--45},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.72.2.28}
}
```
This paper disentangles the competing effects of quality versus productivity orientations on three frontline outcomes — unit revenue, efficiency, and customer satisfaction — using multi-unit field data and simultaneous-equations estimation. It finds the two orientations operate through distinct mechanisms and can trade off, so blanket emphasis on one harms the others. A key reference on managing the quality–productivity tension at the service frontline.
Chapter: service

```bibtex
@article{ye2012bottomup,
  author  = {Ye, Jun and Marinova, Detelina and Singh, Jagdip},
  title   = {Bottom-up Learning in Marketing Frontlines: Conceptualization, Processes, and Consequences},
  journal = {Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {40},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {821--844},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11747-011-0289-7}
}
```
The authors conceptualize "bottom-up learning" — how frontline employees generate and propagate market knowledge upward — and theorize its processes and performance consequences. Using field data, they show bottom-up learning complements top-down direction and improves unit adaptiveness. Central to Marinova's program on organizational learning originating at the customer interface.
Chapter: service

```bibtex
@article{marinova2018frontline,
  author  = {Marinova, Detelina and Singh, Sunil K. and Singh, Jagdip},
  title   = {Frontline Problem-Solving Effectiveness: A Dynamic Analysis of Verbal and Nonverbal Cues},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {55},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {178--192},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.15.0243}
}
```
Coding verbal and nonverbal (vocal/visual) cues from recorded service interactions, the authors model how the temporal dynamics of these cues drive frontline problem-solving effectiveness. They find that not just the presence but the timing and trajectory of cues matter, using dynamic time-series analysis of dyadic encounters. An early demonstration of multimodal behavioral measurement in frontline research.
Chapter: communication

```bibtex
@article{arnold2023disrupting,
  author  = {Arnold, Todd and Marinova, Detelina},
  title   = {Disrupting the Organizational Frontlines},
  journal = {Journal of Service Research},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {26},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {303--309},
  doi     = {10.1177/10946705231175286}
}
```
This editorial/framing piece for a *JSR* special section argues that technological, structural, and workforce disruptions are reconstituting the organizational frontline and sets a research agenda for the disrupted frontline. It synthesizes themes across the section and positions frontline disruption as a core service-research priority. Useful as an agenda-setting citation.
Chapter: service

```bibtex
@article{kramer2026adherence,
  author  = {Kramer, Victoria and Wiebringhaus, Janina and Marinova, Detelina and Krafft, Manfred},
  title   = {Adherence in Mental Healthcare: The Role of Frontline Employee Behaviors},
```

This study examines how frontline employee behaviors in mental-healthcare settings shape patient adherence, a transformative-service outcome. It links specific frontline behaviors to adherence and well-being, extending frontline research into healthcare service contexts. (Published online 31 January 2026; Crossref lists no volume/issue/pages yet.)
Chapter: service

## Knowledge, Learning & Service Innovation

```bibtex
@article{marinova2004actualizing,
  author  = {Marinova, Detelina},
  title   = {Actualizing Innovation Effort: The Impact of Market Knowledge Diffusion in a Dynamic System of Competition},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {68},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {1--20},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.68.3.1.34768}
}
```
Marinova's solo-authored *JM* article models how market knowledge diffuses across competing firms and how that diffusion converts innovation effort into realized innovation outcomes within a dynamic competitive system. Using a dynamic modeling approach, it shows knowledge diffusion both spurs and constrains firms' returns to innovation effort. A seminal early statement of her interest in knowledge and innovation dynamics.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{singh2013stemming,
  author  = {Singh, Jagdip and Marinova, Detelina},
  title   = {Stemming Frontline Performance Losses in Service Innovation Implementation},
  journal = {Marketing Review St. Gallen},
  year    = {2013},
  doi     = {10.1365/s11621-013-0274-2}
}
```
A managerially oriented treatment of why service-innovation rollouts erode frontline performance and how firms can stem those losses. It distills field findings into implementation guidance for managers introducing service innovations at the frontline. A bridge between the authors' academic frontline work and practitioner audiences.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{tang2020lessismore,
  author  = {Tang, Yihui and Marinova, Detelina},
  title   = {When Less is More: The Downside of Customer Knowledge Sharing in New Product Development Teams},
  journal = {Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science},
  year    = {2020},
  volume  = {48},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {288--307},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11747-019-00646-w}
}
```
Challenging the assumption that more customer knowledge is always better, this study shows that excessive customer knowledge sharing in NPD teams can degrade outcomes (an inverted-U relationship). Using team-level data, it identifies conditions under which customer knowledge integration helps versus hurts new-product performance. Connects customer knowledge, teams, and innovation outcomes.
Chapter: innovation

## Sales & Salesforce

```bibtex
@article{chandrashekaran2000threshold,
  author  = {Chandrashekaran, Murali and McNeilly, Kevin and Russ, Frederick A. and Marinova, Detelina},
  title   = {From Uncertain Intentions to Actual Behavior: A Threshold Model of Whether and When Salespeople Quit},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2000},
  volume  = {37},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {463--479},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.37.4.463.18793}
}
```
This paper develops a threshold model linking salespeople's stated turnover intentions to whether and when they actually quit, addressing the well-known intention–behavior gap in salesforce turnover. Using longitudinal salesperson data and hazard-style modeling, it shows intentions translate into exit only past individual thresholds. An early, methodologically notable salesforce-turnover contribution.
Chapter: sales

```bibtex
@article{singh2018query,
  author  = {Singh, Sunil and Marinova, Detelina and Singh, Jagdip and Evans, Kenneth R.},
  title   = {Customer Query Handling in Sales Interactions},
  journal = {Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {46},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {837--856},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11747-017-0569-y}
}
```
The authors study how salespeople handle customer queries during live sales interactions and how query-handling behaviors affect interaction outcomes. Drawing on coded interaction data, they identify effective query-handling strategies and their boundary conditions. Bridges salesforce behavior with the micro-dynamics of customer conversations.
Chapter: sales

```bibtex
@article{singh2020enegotiations,
  author  = {Singh, Sunil K. and Marinova, Detelina and Singh, Jagdip},
  title   = {Business-to-Business E-Negotiations and Influence Tactics},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2020},
  volume  = {84},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {47--68},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022242919899381}
}
```
Analyzing the text of B2B e-negotiations, the authors examine how salespeople's influence tactics, expressed through written cues, shape negotiation outcomes. They apply text analysis to email negotiation data, linking specific tactical language to deal performance. A key sales paper combining negotiation theory with textual/computational measurement.
Chapter: sales

## Customer Engagement, Retention & Channels

```bibtex
@article{lund2014revenue,
  author  = {Lund, Donald J. and Marinova, Detelina},
  title   = {Managing Revenue across Retail Channels: The Interplay of Service Performance and Direct Marketing},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {78},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {99--118},
  doi     = {10.1509/jm.13.0220}
}
```
Using 223 weeks of multi-store data from a major retailer, the study shows that objective service performance and direct-marketing investments interact differently across on-site versus remote retail delivery channels. Servicescape perceptions moderate the service–marketing trade-off, and the channel determines which perceptions matter. A modeling-heavy contribution on coordinating service and marketing across channels.
Chapter: modeling

```bibtex
@article{marinova2014membership,
  author  = {Marinova, Detelina and Singh, Jagdip},
  title   = {Consumer Decision to Upgrade or Downgrade a Service Membership},
  journal = {Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {42},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {596--618},
  doi     = {10.1007/s11747-014-0374-9}
}
```
This paper models the consumer's decision to upgrade or downgrade a service membership tier, treating up- and down-grade as asymmetric, distinct choices rather than mirror images. Using customer panel data, it identifies the drivers that push members in each direction. Relevant to subscription/membership retention and customer-base management.
Chapter: satisfaction

```bibtex
@article{hu2025dei,
  author  = {Hu, Lingshu and Zhang, Weilu and Marinova, Detelina},

  number  = {1},
```

Using deep-learning classification of large-scale social-media data, the authors assess how political ideology and brand type jointly shape consumer engagement with brands' DEI initiatives. They find engagement varies systematically with ideological alignment and brand positioning. A recent example of Marinova's computational/text-and-image analysis applied to brand–consumer engagement.

This conceptual article theorizes how technology-empowered frontline interactions generate learning for firms, customers, and employees, framing "smart" service as a learning system rather than mere automation.
