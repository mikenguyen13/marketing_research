## Brand Purpose, Activism, and CSR

### Bhagwat, Warren, Beck & Watson (2020) — Corporate Sociopolitical Activism and Firm Value

```bibtex
@article{bhagwat2020activism,
  author  = {Bhagwat, Yashoda and Warren, Nooshin L. and Beck, Joshua T. and Watson, George F.},
  title   = {Corporate Sociopolitical Activism and Firm Value},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2020},
  volume  = {84},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {1--21},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022242920937000}
}
```

Defines corporate sociopolitical activism (CSA) — public stances on contested social-political issues — as distinct from CSR, and uses an event study of 293 CSA events to estimate stock-market reactions. Finds that CSA, on average, elicits a negative investor response, with magnitude moderated by issue framing, customer-base political alignment, and whether the stance aligns with prevailing norms. A foundational empirical reference for the firm-value consequences of "brands taking a stand."
Section: Brand Valuation (also Reputation and Evaluation)

### Nickerson, Lowe, Pattabhiramaiah & Sorescu (2021) — CSR and Brand Sales

```bibtex
@article{nickerson2021csr,
  author  = {Nickerson, Dionne and Lowe, Michael and Pattabhiramaiah, Adithya and Sorescu, Alina},
  title   = {The Impact of Corporate Social Responsibility on Brand Sales: An Accountability Perspective},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2021},
  volume  = {86},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {5--28},
  doi     = {10.1177/00222429211044155}
}
```

Examines how CSR initiatives translate into brand sales through an "accountability" lens, using longitudinal CPG data. Shows that the sales payoff to CSR is conditional: consumers reward CSR more when the firm is held publicly accountable for its claims (e.g., third-party certification, transparency), tying brand-level CSR to measurable demand outcomes rather than attitudes alone.
Section: Brand Equity (also Reputation and Evaluation)

## Brand Authenticity

### Zhang & Patrick (2021) — Brand Nicknames and Authenticity

```bibtex
@article{zhang2021nickname,
  author  = {Zhang, Zhe and Patrick, Vanessa M.},
  title   = {Mickey D's Has More Street Cred Than McDonald's: Consumer Brand Nickname Use Signals Information Authenticity},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2021},
  volume  = {85},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {58--73},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022242921996277}
}
```

Across experiments, shows that consumer-generated brand nicknames (e.g., "Mickey D's") signal information authenticity, lending credibility to claims made about the brand. The "in-group" linguistic informality cues that the message comes from a genuine insider, increasing persuasion. Demonstrates a linguistic, bottom-up route to perceived authenticity distinct from firm-controlled brand language.
Section: Brand Authenticity (also Brand Elements)

### Biraglia, Fuchs, Maira & Puntoni (2023) — Brand Acquisitions and Values Authenticity

```bibtex
@article{biraglia2023acquisitions,
  author  = {Biraglia, Alessandro and Fuchs, Christoph and Maira, Elisa and Puntoni, Stefano},
  title   = {When and Why Consumers React Negatively to Brand Acquisitions: A Values Authenticity Account},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {87},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {509--528},
  doi     = {10.1177/00222429221137817}
}
```

Develops a "values authenticity" account of why consumers sometimes punish a brand after it is acquired by a larger parent. Through experiments, shows negative reactions arise when the acquisition is perceived to threaten the brand's original values, mediated by perceived loss of authenticity; effects attenuate when value continuity is signaled. Bridges authenticity and brand-architecture literatures.
Section: Brand Authenticity (also Brand Architecture and Portfolio)

## Brand Coolness, Personality, and Anthropomorphism

### Warren, Batra, Loureiro & Bagozzi (2019) — Brand Coolness

```bibtex
@article{warren2019coolness,
  author  = {Warren, Caleb and Batra, Rajeev and Loureiro, Sandra Maria Correia and Bagozzi, Richard P.},
  title   = {Brand Coolness},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2019},
  volume  = {83},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {36--56},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022242919857698}
}
```

Conceptualizes and measures "brand coolness" as a perceived brand quality, identifying ten characteristics (e.g., autonomous, rebellious, authentic, original, aesthetically appealing) across multi-study scale development. Shows coolness drives brand attitude, willingness to pay, and word of mouth, and distinguishes two types (countercultural vs. mainstream cool). A frequently cited modern addition to brand personality/associations.
Section: Brand Equity → Associations, Meaning, Image, Personality, and Coolness

### Puzakova & Aggarwal (2018) — Brands as Rivals (Anthropomorphism)

```bibtex
@article{puzakova2018rivals,
  author  = {Puzakova, Marina and Aggarwal, Pankaj},
  title   = {Brands as Rivals: Consumer Pursuit of Distinctiveness and the Role of Brand Anthropomorphism},
  journal = {Journal of Consumer Research},
  year    = {2018},
  volume  = {45},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {869--888},
  doi     = {10.1093/jcr/ucy035}
}
```

Shows that anthropomorphizing a brand can backfire when consumers pursue distinctiveness: a humanlike brand is treated as a social rival, so consumers seeking to stand out evaluate it less favorably and switch away. Establishes a boundary condition on the generally positive effects of brand anthropomorphism via experiments manipulating distinctiveness motives.
Section: Brand Equity → Associations, Meaning, Image, Personality, and Coolness (also Brand–Consumer Interaction)

## AI, Algorithms, and Voice in Branding

### Puntoni, Reczek, Giesler & Botti (2021) — Consumers and AI: An Experiential Perspective

```bibtex
@article{puntoni2021ai,

  volume  = {85},
```

A conceptual framework mapping how consumers experience AI-enabled brand interactions across data capture, classification, delegation, and social experiences, each with both enabling and threatening facets. ### Longoni & Cian (2022) — The "Word-of-Machine" Effect

```bibtex
@article{longoni2022wordofmachine,
  author  = {Longoni, Chiara and Cian, Luca},
  title   = {Artificial Intelligence in Utilitarian vs. Hedonic Contexts: The ``Word-of-Machine'' Effect},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2022},
  volume  = {86},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {91--108},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022242920957347}
}
```

Identifies the "word-of-machine" effect: consumers prefer AI (vs. human) recommenders for utilitarian attributes but human recommenders for hedonic/experiential attributes, because algorithms are stereotyped as competent-but-not-experiential. Demonstrated across experiments with downstream effects on choice and brand recommendation acceptance. (Online-first 2020; version-of-record vol. 86, 2022.)
Section: Brand–Consumer Interaction

### Melumad (2023) — Vocalizing Search (Voice Technologies)

```bibtex
@article{melumad2023vocalizing,
```

## Logo, Visual Identity, and Sound Symbolism (Computational and Experimental)

```bibtex
@article{luffarelli2019asymmetry,
```

## Brand Equity, Measurement, and Firm Value

### Datta, Ailawadi & van Heerde (2017) — Consumer- vs. Sales-Based Brand Equity

```bibtex
@article{datta2017equityalign,
  author  = {Datta, Hannes and Ailawadi, Kusum L. and van Heerde, Harald J.},
  title   = {How Well Does Consumer-Based Brand Equity Align with Sales-Based Brand Equity and Marketing-Mix Response?},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2017},
  volume  = {81},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {1--20},
  doi     = {10.1509/jm.15.0340}
}
```

Empirically reconciles survey-based consumer brand equity with sales-based (revenue-premium) brand equity across many brands and categories. Finds the two are correlated but diverge meaningfully, and that consumer-based equity predicts marketing-mix response elasticities. A key reference for the reflective-vs-formative and measurement debates in the equity subsection.
Section: Brand Equity → Measuring Equity (and Is Equity Reflective or Formative?)

## Reputation, Crisis, Firestorms, and Brand Safety


Conceptualizes and measures "brand corporateness" — the perception that a brand is impersonal/profit-driven "big corporate" — as an unfavorable association, and shows it predicts reduced support relative to brands seen as more human/local. Scale-development plus experiments; relevant to authenticity, anthropomorphism, and the "big brand" penalty. (Online-first Nov 2024; vol. 52, 2025 print.)
Section: Brand Equity → Associations, Meaning, Image, Personality (also Brand Authenticity)
