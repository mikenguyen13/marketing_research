## Advertising effectiveness & elasticity

```bibtex
@article{tellis1988advertising,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Advertising Exposure, Loyalty, and Brand Purchase: A Two-Stage Model of Choice},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1988},
  volume  = {25},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {134--144},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224378802500202}
}
```
Using single-source scanner-panel data for refrigerated orange juice, Tellis estimates a two-stage logit model separating the decision to buy the category from the choice of brand. He finds that brand loyalty (past purchase) dominates choice, while advertising exposure has a relatively small effect on brand selection and works mainly by reinforcing/maintaining the loyal base rather than winning switchers. A foundational empirical statement on the limited persuasive power of advertising at the brand-choice margin.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{tellis1988price,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {The Price Elasticity of Selective Demand: A Meta-Analysis of Econometric Models of Sales},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1988},
  volume  = {25},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {331--341},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224378802500401}
}
```
A meta-analysis synthesizing 367 price-elasticity estimates drawn from roughly 220 brands/markets across prior econometric sales models. Tellis reports that mean selective (brand-level) price elasticity is strongly negative and far larger in magnitude than advertising elasticity, and he models how methodological factors (model form, data type, product category) systematically shift estimates. A canonical empirical-generalization paper on price sensitivity.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{tellis2000whichad,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J. and Chandy, Rajesh K. and Thaivanich, Pattana},
  title   = {Which Ad Works, When, Where, and How Often? Modeling the Effects of Direct Television Advertising},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2000},
  volume  = {37},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {32--46},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.37.1.32.18716}
}
```
Using hourly direct-response data for a toll-free medical referral service, the authors build a model identifying which specific ad executions drive response, at which stations/times, and with what carryover decay. They find advertising effects are short-lived (carryover measured in hours/days), that the choice of ad and airing context matters more than sheer frequency, and that wearout sets in quickly. A landmark single-source "what works when" advertising study.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{chandy2001whattosay,
  author  = {Chandy, Rajesh K. and Tellis, Gerard J. and MacInnis, Deborah J. and Thaivanich, Pattana},
  title   = {What to Say When: Advertising Appeals in Evolving Markets},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2001},
  volume  = {38},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {399--414},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.38.4.399.18908}
}
```
Building on the same direct-response data stream, the authors show that the effectiveness of different advertising appeals (argument vs. emotion/affect) depends on the market's stage of evolution. Argument- and expert-based appeals work better in newer markets, whereas emotional cues and trustworthiness become more effective as markets mature. Connects creative content to market lifecycle.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{sethuraman2011advertising,
  author  = {Sethuraman, Raj and Tellis, Gerard J. and Briesch, Richard A.},
  title   = {How Well Does Advertising Work? Generalizations from Meta-Analysis of Brand Advertising Elasticities},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2011},
  volume  = {48},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {457--471},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkr.48.3.457}
}
```
A large meta-analysis of 751 short-term and 402 long-term brand advertising elasticities. The authors find the average short-term advertising elasticity is about 0.12 — substantially below the prior meta-analytic mean of 0.22 — and that elasticities are higher for durables, in early lifecycle stages, and in print. The benchmark empirical generalization on how (weakly) advertising moves sales. (Published JMR version of record; supersedes the SSRN preprint 10.2139/ssrn.1866002.)
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{sethuraman2002manufacturer,
  author  = {Sethuraman, Raj and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Does Manufacturer Advertising Suppress or Stimulate Retail Price Promotions? Analytical Model and Empirical Analysis},
  journal = {Journal of Retailing},
  year    = {2002},
  volume  = {78},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {253--263},
  doi     = {10.1016/S0022-4359(02)00066-0}
}
```
Combines an analytical model with empirical analysis to ask whether manufacturer pull advertising and retailer price promotions are substitutes or complements. The authors derive conditions under which advertising suppresses versus stimulates promotional discounting, clarifying the strategic interaction between national advertising and in-store price cuts. Bridges advertising and pricing.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{tellis2009generalizations,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Generalizations about Advertising Effectiveness in Markets},
  journal = {Journal of Advertising Research},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {49},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {240--245},
  doi     = {10.2501/S0021849909090357}
}
```
A concise review article distilling decades of empirical findings into stated generalizations about when and how advertising affects sales. Tellis emphasizes that advertising elasticity is small, carryover is short, frequency effects are modest, and content/targeting matter more than raw spending. A useful capstone summary for citing the "what we know about advertising" consensus.
Chapter: advertising

```bibtex
@article{tellis2009recession,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J. and Tellis, Kethan},
  title   = {Research on Advertising in a Recession: A Critical Review and Synthesis},
  journal = {Journal of Advertising Research},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {49},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {304--327},
  doi     = {10.2501/S0021849909090400}
}
```
A critical review synthesizing the literature on advertising during economic downturns. The authors weigh evidence on whether maintaining or cutting advertising in recessions pays off, summarizing methodological challenges and the (mixed but generally pro-investment) findings. Relevant for budgeting-under-uncertainty discussions.
Chapter: advertising

## Pricing, quality & value

```bibtex
@article{tellis1986price,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Beyond the Many Faces of Price: An Integration of Pricing Strategies},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {1986},
  volume  = {50},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {146--160},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224298605000402}
}
```
A conceptual synthesis offering a unifying taxonomy of pricing strategies organized by the economies shared among buyer segments, the firm, and product lines (e.g., random/periodic discounting, second-market discounting, price signaling, penetration, image pricing, bundling). Widely cited as the organizing framework for differential and competitive pricing tactics.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{tellis1987competitive,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J. and Wernerfelt, Birger},
  title   = {Competitive Price and Quality Under Asymmetric Information},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {1987},
  volume  = {6},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {240--253},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.6.3.240}
}
```
An analytical and empirical study of how the price–quality correlation depends on the level of consumer information in a market. The authors show, and corroborate with data, that the equilibrium correlation between price and quality generally increases as market information improves, explaining why price is a weak quality signal in poorly informed markets. Foundational for value/quality inference.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{tellis1990bestvalue,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J. and Gaeth, Gary J.},
  title   = {Best Value, Price-Seeking, and Price Aversion: The Impact of Information and Learning on Consumer Choices},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {1990},
  volume  = {54},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {34--45},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224299005400203}
}
```
An experimental study identifying three consumer strategies under price/quality uncertainty: best-value seeking, price-seeking (inferring quality from high price), and price aversion (buying cheapest). The authors show how information provision and learning shift consumers toward best-value choices, with implications for how uncertainty drives seemingly irrational price reliance.
Chapter: branding

```bibtex
@article{stremersch2002bundling,
  author  = {Stremersch, Stefan and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Strategic Bundling of Products and Prices: A New Synthesis for Marketing},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2002},
  volume  = {66},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {55--72},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.66.1.55.18455}
}
```
A conceptual synthesis that disentangles product bundling from price bundling and proposes a clear typology (pure vs. mixed bundling; product vs. price bundling). The authors clarify terminology, review legality and strategy, and propose research generalizations, giving the field a common framework for analyzing bundling decisions.
Chapter: branding

## New-product growth, diffusion & innovation

```bibtex
@article{golder1993pioneer,
  author  = {Golder, Peter N. and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Pioneer Advantage: Marketing Logic or Marketing Legend?},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1993},
  volume  = {30},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {158--170},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224379303000203}
}
```
Using historical (rather than survivor-biased) data on 500 brands across 50 product categories, the authors overturn the received wisdom that market pioneers enjoy durable advantage. They find that almost half of pioneers fail and that early market leaders (entering on average about a decade after pioneers) tend to dominate long-term. A landmark debunking of first-mover advantage.
Chapter: market-entry

```bibtex
@article{golder1997takeoff,
  author  = {Golder, Peter N. and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Will It Ever Fly? Modeling the Takeoff of Really New Consumer Durables},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {1997},
  volume  = {16},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {256--270},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.16.3.256}
}
```
Introduces the concept and measurement of "takeoff" — the dramatic sales surge separating introduction from growth — and models it with a hazard framework across many consumer durables. The authors find takeoff is strongly tied to falling price (price at takeoff averages roughly 63% of the introductory price) and that time-to-takeoff has shortened over the decades. Foundational for product-lifecycle timing.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{chandy1998cannibalize,
  author  = {Chandy, Rajesh K. and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Organizing for Radical Product Innovation: The Overlooked Role of Willingness to Cannibalize},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {1998},
  volume  = {35},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {474--487},
  doi     = {10.1177/002224379803500406}
}
```
Survey-based study arguing that a firm's willingness to cannibalize its own existing investments — more than size or other structural factors — drives radical product innovation. The authors develop and test the construct, finding it (along with future-market focus and risk tolerance) to be a key organizational antecedent of breakthrough innovation.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{chandy2000incumbents,
  author  = {Chandy, Rajesh K. and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {The Incumbent's Curse? Incumbency, Size, and Radical Product Innovation},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2000},
  volume  = {64},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {1--17},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.64.3.1.18033}
}
```
Using a historical database of radical innovations in consumer durables and office products, the authors challenge the assumption that large incumbents rarely innovate radically. They find incumbents and large firms have introduced a substantial share of radical innovations, especially since WWII, undercutting the "incumbent's curse" as a general law. A key empirical-generalization paper on innovation and firm size.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{golder2004cascades,
  author  = {Golder, Peter N. and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Growing, Growing, Gone: Cascades, Diffusion, and Turning Points in the Product Life Cycle},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {23},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {207--218},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1040.0057}
}
```
Analyzes the full product life cycle of consumer durables, identifying systematic turning points — takeoff into growth and a subsequent "slowdown" / temporary sales decline ("saddle") at the transition to maturity. The authors document an information-cascade explanation for these turning points, giving managers empirical benchmarks for life-cycle phases. Extends takeoff research to the growth-to-maturity transition.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{sood2005technological,
  author  = {Sood, Ashish and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Technological Evolution and Radical Innovation},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2005},
  volume  = {69},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {152--168},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.69.3.152.66361}
}
```
Empirically examines how technology performance evolves, challenging the standard single S-curve view. Across multiple markets the authors find a step-function pattern — sharp jumps in performance after long plateaus — and that new technologies frequently arrive below, not above, incumbents, and from outside the industry. Reframes how radical technological change actually unfolds.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{prabhu2005acquisitions,
  author  = {Prabhu, Jaideep C. and Chandy, Rajesh K. and Ellis, Mark E.},
  title   = {The Impact of Acquisitions on Innovation: Poison Pill, Placebo, or Tonic?},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2005},
  volume  = {69},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {114--130},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.69.1.114.55514}
}
```
Studies pharmaceutical acquisitions (1988–1997) to ask whether buying firms helps or hurts subsequent innovation. The authors find that acquisitions can act as a "tonic" for innovation when the acquirer has a strong internal base of knowledge to absorb and combine with the target's, rather than a uniform "poison pill." (Tellis-circle co-authors; central to the innovation-via-M&A discussion.)
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{tellis2009radical,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J. and Prabhu, Jaideep C. and Chandy, Rajesh K.},
  title   = {Radical Innovation across Nations: The Preeminence of Corporate Culture},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {73},
  number  = {1},
  pages   = {3--23},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmkg.73.1.003}
}
```
Survey of 759 firms across 17 major economies testing what drives radical innovation. The headline finding is that internal corporate culture (future orientation, risk tolerance, willingness to cannibalize, incentives, empowerment) is the strongest driver of radical innovation — outweighing national labor, capital, and government factors. A major cross-national innovation study.
Chapter: innovation

```bibtex
@article{sood2009innovations,
  author  = {Sood, Ashish and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Do Innovations Really Pay Off? Total Stock Market Returns to Innovation},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2009},
  volume  = {28},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {442--456},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1080.0407}
}
```
Develops an event-study framework measuring stock-market returns across the full chain of innovation events (from initiation through commercialization). The authors find the total market value created by an innovation project (about $643M) is many times the reaction to an average single event, and that markets reward competitors' failures as well as own successes. A key innovation-and-firm-value paper.
Chapter: metrics

```bibtex
@article{sood2012saw,
  author  = {Sood, Ashish and James, Gareth M. and Tellis, Gerard J. and Zhu, Ji},
  title   = {Predicting the Path of Technological Innovation: SAW vs. Moore, Bass, Gompertz, and Kryder},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {31},
  number  = {6},
  pages   = {964--979},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1120.0739}
}
```
Proposes a "Step-And-Wait" (SAW) model of technological performance improvement and benchmarks its predictive accuracy against Moore's Law, the Bass model, Gompertz, and Kryder's Law across many technologies. SAW better captures the lumpy step-then-plateau dynamics of performance and forecasts future technology paths more accurately. A modeling contribution for technology forecasting.
Chapter: modeling

```bibtex
@incollection{chandrasekaran2007diffusion,
  author    = {Chandrasekaran, Deepa and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title     = {A Critical Review of Marketing Research on Diffusion of New Products},
  booktitle = {Review of Marketing Research},
  publisher = {Emerald Group Publishing},
  year      = {2007},
  volume    = {3},
  pages     = {39--80},
  doi       = {10.1108/S1548-6435(2007)0000003006}
}
```
A comprehensive review chapter synthesizing the new-product diffusion literature — the Bass model and its extensions, empirical generalizations on adoption, takeoff and saddles, and cross-country diffusion. Widely used as the authoritative survey of what diffusion research has established. A go-to citation for diffusion overviews.
Chapter: innovation

## International diffusion & takeoff

```bibtex
@article{tellis2003international,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J. and Stremersch, Stefan and Yin, Eden},
  title   = {The International Takeoff of New Products: The Role of Economics, Culture, and Country Innovativeness},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2003},
  volume  = {22},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {188--208},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.22.2.188.16041}
}
```
Examines 137 new products across 10 categories and 16 European countries to explain why takeoff happens sooner in some countries than others. The authors find large cross-country differences driven by economic wealth and especially culture (e.g., a country's "roles of work and play"), with Northern European/Scandinavian markets taking off faster. A foundational international-diffusion study.
Chapter: international

```bibtex
@article{stremersch2004managing,
  author  = {Stremersch, Stefan and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Understanding and Managing International Growth of New Products},
  journal = {International Journal of Research in Marketing},
  year    = {2004},
  volume  = {21},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {421--438},
  doi     = {10.1016/j.ijresmar.2004.07.001}
}
```
Analyzes the growth (not just takeoff) stage of new products across European countries, decomposing international growth into its drivers and the "waterfall vs. sprinkler" sequencing of cross-country rollout. Provides managerial guidance on staging international launches and on which growth patterns generalize. Complements the international-takeoff work.
Chapter: international

```bibtex
@article{chandrasekaran2008global,
  author  = {Chandrasekaran, Deepa and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Global Takeoff of New Products: Culture, Wealth, or Vanishing Differences?},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2008},
  volume  = {27},
  number  = {5},
  pages   = {844--860},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1070.0329}
}
```
Studies takeoff of multiple new products across 31 countries to test whether cross-country differences in adoption are persistent or shrinking. The authors find evidence of "vanishing differences" — a convergence in time-to-takeoff over time and across countries — alongside enduring roles for wealth and culture. Important for globalization-of-diffusion arguments.
Chapter: international

## Online video virality & text/UGC analytics

```bibtex
@article{tirunillai2012chatter,
  author  = {Tirunillai, Seshadri and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Does Chatter Really Matter? Dynamics of User-Generated Content and Stock Performance},
  journal = {Marketing Science},
  year    = {2012},
  volume  = {31},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {198--215},
  doi     = {10.1287/mksc.1110.0682}
}
```
Links the volume and valence of online user-generated content (product reviews/chatter) to firm stock performance using vector-autoregression on a large multi-market dataset. The authors find that chatter volume leads abnormal stock returns, that negative valence has a stronger and faster effect than positive, and that UGC has predictive value for firm value. A foundational social-media-metrics paper.
Chapter: metrics

```bibtex
@article{tirunillai2014mining,
  author  = {Tirunillai, Seshadri and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Mining Marketing Meaning from Online Chatter: Strategic Brand Analysis of Big Data Using Latent Dirichlet Allocation},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing Research},
  year    = {2014},
  volume  = {51},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {463--479},
  doi     = {10.1509/jmr.12.0106}
}
```
Applies unsupervised Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) to a large corpus of consumer reviews to extract latent dimensions of satisfaction and map competitive brand positioning from text. The framework recovers interpretable quality dimensions, their valence/labels, and brand strengths/weaknesses without predefined attributes. A foundational text-mining-for-marketing method paper.
Chapter: metrics

```bibtex
@article{tellis2019virality,
  author  = {Tellis, Gerard J. and MacInnis, Deborah J. and Tirunillai, Seshadri and Zhang, Yanwei},
  title   = {What Drives Virality (Sharing) of Online Digital Content? The Critical Role of Information, Emotion, and Brand Prominence},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2019},
  volume  = {83},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {1--20},
  doi     = {10.1177/0022242919841034}
}
```
Analyzes sharing of online (YouTube) video ads to identify what makes content go viral. The authors find that positive emotions (amusement, excitement, inspiration, warmth) increase sharing, that information content can help, and — counterintuitively — that prominent/early brand placement reduces sharing. A leading empirical study of advertising virality.
Chapter: virality

```bibtex
@article{wei2022machinelearning,
  author  = {Wei, Yanhao and Hong, Jihoon and Tellis, Gerard J.},
  title   = {Machine Learning for Creativity: Using Similarity Networks to Design Better Crowdfunding Projects},
  journal = {Journal of Marketing},
  year    = {2022},
  volume  = {86},
  number  = {2},
  pages   = {87--104},
  doi     = {10.1177/00222429211005481}
}
```
Uses machine learning (Word2Vec embeddings and Word Mover's Distance) to measure the similarity of Kickstarter project descriptions and relate creativity/distinctiveness to funding success. The authors show that moderate novelty relative to peers helps, and demonstrate how similarity networks can guide the design of better crowdfunding pitches. A recent ML-meets-marketing contribution.
Chapter: modeling

## UNVERIFIED — do not cite

The following frequently appear in citations of Tellis's work but were only located under **SSRN/preprint or non-version-of-record DOIs** in this verification pass, OR their published DOI was not confirmed here. Do NOT cite from this list until a published DOI is verified.

- **"What Drives Innovation in Firms Across Nations? A Culture of Innovation."** Tellis, Prabhu, Chandy. Only the SSRN preprint DOI (10.2139/ssrn.961477) was found; this appears to be the working-paper precursor to the published *Journal of Marketing* (2009) "Radical Innovation across Nations" article above — cite that published version (10.1509/jmkg.73.1.003) instead.
- **"Pricing in the International Takeoff of New Products."** Chandrasekaran, Arts, Tellis, Frambach. Only an SSRN DOI (10.2139/ssrn.2165263) was found in this pass; locate and verify the published *IJRM* version-of-record DOI before citing.
- **"The S-Curve of Technological Evolution: Marketing Law or Self-Fulfilling Prophecy?"** Sood, Tellis. Only an SSRN DOI (10.2139/ssrn.981532) was found; confirm whether a published journal version exists before citing.
- **"Stock Market Returns to Financial Innovations Before and During the Financial Crisis."** Schöler, Skiera, Tellis. A *Journal of Product Innovation Management* DOI (10.1111/jpim.12138) was returned by Crossref but the full metadata (exact pages/volume) was not independently re-resolved in this pass — verify directly before citing.
