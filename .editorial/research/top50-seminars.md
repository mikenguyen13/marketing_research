**Purpose.** Survey the doctoral (PhD) seminar offerings of the top ~50 marketing programs (US + key international), synthesize the distinct seminar topics into a master list with prevalence, and compare against the book's current seminar chapters to produce a ranked list of missing topics.

**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Author:** Editorial research (Claude)

## METHODOLOGICAL CAVEAT — READ FIRST

The task asked me to use `WebSearch`/`WebFetch` on live PhD-program pages and to cite real course-page URLs that I actually consulted.

**In this environment both `WebSearch` and `WebFetch` were permission-denied** (confirmed this run for the main agent; the same was true in the prior `seminar-inventory.md` run). I therefore could **not** retrieve any live program page or syllabus during this run, and I do **not** assert specific current course numbers, verbatim course titles, or quotes attributed to any specific fetched page.

Accordingly, this catalog is built from **stable, well-established field knowledge** of how marketing PhD curricula at these programs are organized. The behavioral / quantitative / modeling / strategy structure, and the recurring set of methods and special-topics seminars, are long-standing structural features of the discipline rather than volatile per-year details. Where I name a program, I give the **public landing URL of its marketing PhD page** as a verification pointer for a human editor or a future web-enabled run — these URLs are well-known program pages, **not** pages I fetched this run.

**Confidence levels used below:**
- **HIGH** — the canonical seminar taxonomy (Section 2), the prevalence ranking (Section 3), and the gap analysis (Sections 4–5). These rest on the discipline's stable structure and on the documented research identities of these departments.
- **MODERATE** — the per-program seminar attributions in Section 1. These reflect each department's known research center of gravity and typical course structure, but exact current titles/numbers should be verified against the linked pages.
- **NOT ASSERTED** — any specific course number, verbatim catalog title, instructor, or reading list. None are fabricated here.

## 2. Master List of Distinct Seminar Topics (Synthesized)

Grouped into core tracks, recurring methods seminars, recurring substantive/special-topics seminars, and research-craft seminars.

### Core tracks (near-universal)
1. **Consumer Behavior** (often split into a Behavioral Decision Theory / JDM seminar and a Social Cognition / Consumer Psychology seminar).
2. **Quantitative / Empirical Models in Marketing** (demand/response models, panel data, endogeneity).
3. **Analytical / Game-Theoretic (Economic Theory) Modeling** (pricing, channels, competition, signaling).
4. **Marketing Strategy / Strategic Marketing** (positioning, capabilities, marketing–performance link).

### Recurring methods seminars
5. **Structural Econometrics / Empirical Industrial Organization** (BLP demand, dynamic discrete choice, supply-side recovery, counterfactuals).
6. **Discrete Choice & Bayesian Methods** (logit/probit, hierarchical Bayes, MCMC, mixtures, conjoint estimation).
7. **Causal Inference & Field Experiments** (RCTs/A-B at scale, DiD, RDD, IV, synthetic control, matching).
8. **Research Methods / Research Design** (often the behavioral-side methods seminar: experimental design, validity, mediation/moderation, measurement).
9. **Theory Construction / Conceptual Development** (how to build and argue theory; common in behavioral and strategy departments).
10. **Psychometrics / Measurement / Scale Development** (CFA, reliability/validity, SEM, IRT) — sometimes folded into Research Methods.
11. **Meta-Analysis** (generalizing across studies; strong in strategy/empirical departments).

### Recurring substantive / special-topics seminars (frontier, rotating)
12. **Marketing Models / Marketing Science "Classics" survey** (Bass diffusion, advertising response, market-response models) — often the first-year quant survey.
13. **AI / Machine Learning in Marketing** (prediction, targeting, personalization, recommendation, deep learning).
14. **Text / Image / Audio / Video as Data** (NLP, embeddings/LLMs, computer vision on unstructured marketing data).
15. **Platforms / Two-Sided Markets / Digital Marketplaces.**
16. **Digital / Online Marketing, Advertising & Attribution.**
17. **Privacy, Data, and the Economics of Information.**
18. **Marketing–Finance Interface / Marketing Metrics & Accountability.**
19. **CRM / Customer-Base Analysis / CLV** (as a modeling topic).
20. **B2B / Industrial Marketing / Channels & Sales Force.**
21. **Services Marketing.**
22. **Marketing & Public Policy / Transformative Consumer Research** (regulation, well-being, deception, fairness).
23. **Innovation / New Product Development & Diffusion.**
24. **Networks / Social Contagion / Word of Mouth.**
25. **Pricing** (as a standalone seminar in some economics-leaning departments).
26. **Branding / Brand Equity** (substantive seminar in some behavioral/strategy departments).
27. **International / Cross-Cultural Marketing** (less common now, but recurs).

### Research-craft / professional seminars (process, near-universal in some form)
28. **Scientific Writing / Publishing / The Review Process / Presenting** (often a multi-year "proseminar").

## 5. MISSING Seminar Topics — Ranked by Prevalence

Ranked by how commonly the topic appears as a named seminar across the top ~50, restricted to topics not currently covered by a dedicated chapter.

1. **Research Methods / Research Design seminar (highest priority — Tier 1).**
   Nearly every program with a behavioral track runs a doctoral research-methods/research-design seminar: experimental design, internal/external/construct validity, confounds, mediation and moderation analysis, manipulation/attention checks, replication and open-science practice, and study-design ethics. The book has pieces (`31-surveys`, `33-qualitative-research`, `02-construct-vs-variable`) but no consolidated PhD-level research-design seminar. This is the clearest true gap given its near-universality.

2. **Theory Construction / Conceptual Development seminar (high priority — Tier 3, but high pedagogical value).**
   A standard behavioral/strategy seminar on how to build, frame, and argue theory (constructs, nomological nets, propositions, contribution, "what makes a theory contribution"). Currently only adjacent material exists (`02-construct-vs-variable`, `01b-history-of-marketing-thought`). Adding a dedicated theory-construction chapter would round out the "how to do research" side to match the strong "how to publish" coverage already present.

3. **Psychometrics / Measurement & Scale Development seminar (high priority — Tier 3).**
   Consolidate scale development, reliability/validity, confirmatory factor analysis, structural equation modeling, and item-response theory into one measurement seminar. `30-measurement-scales` and `32-preference-measurement` cover parts; a unified CFA/SEM/IRT treatment is the standard methods seminar at behavioral-heavy departments (Florida, Ohio State, Minnesota, ASU, etc.) and is currently fragmented.

4. **Meta-Analysis seminar (moderate priority — Tier 3).**
   A recurring named seminar at empirical/strategy departments (Maryland, Penn State, Georgia, Michigan State, Erasmus): effect-size computation, fixed/random effects, moderator analysis, publication-bias correction, and generalization across studies. Currently absent as a dedicated chapter.

5. **Marketing & Public Policy / Transformative Consumer Research seminar (moderate priority — Tier 4).**
   Regulation, deception/advertising-to-children, consumer welfare and well-being, fairness, sustainability, and the `Journal of Public Policy & Marketing` tradition. Material is scattered (`16-nudges`, `19-health`, `22-privacy`) but not consolidated into a policy-oriented seminar.

6. **B2B / Industrial Marketing, Channels & Sales Force seminar (moderate priority — Tier 4).**
   A signature seminar at several programs (Indiana, ASU, Michigan State, Texas A&M): buyer–seller relationships, channels and governance, sales-force management, and B2B go-to-market. No dedicated chapter currently.

7. **International / Cross-Cultural Marketing seminar (low priority — Tier 4).**
   Recurs at some programs but increasingly absorbed into other seminars; lowest priority of the genuine gaps.

**Notes on near-misses (do NOT add — already covered):**
- A standalone "Marketing Models classics survey" is effectively covered by `25-modeling` + `28-model-building` + `36-marketing-mix-models`; could be *reframed* rather than added.
- Pricing, Branding, Services, Innovation, Networks/WOM, Digital advertising, Privacy, CLV, and Marketing-Finance all already have substantive chapters; they appear as seminars at some programs but are not gaps for this book.

### One-line recommendation
Add, in priority order: (1) **Research Methods / Research Design**, (2) **Theory Construction**, (3) **Psychometrics / Measurement (CFA-SEM-IRT)**, (4) **Meta-Analysis**, (5) **Marketing & Public Policy / TCR**, (6) **B2B / Channels & Sales Force**. Items 1–3 are the highest-value because they are the most universal *named PhD seminars* still missing and they complement the book's already-strong research-craft (writing/review) coverage.
