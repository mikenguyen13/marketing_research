# Weekly research digest — 2026-08-05

## Summary

Baseline run of the weekly scan: 14 journals swept via Crossref for work published since 2026-07-22, 45 candidates after removing everything already cited in the book (~1,600 known DOIs). **12 kept.**

The week has a clear through-line: **neural architectures are arriving inside choice and demand models, not beside them.** Three separate papers (Marketing Science, Management Science ×1, plus a Marketing Letters retrospective) treat deep learning as a way to *represent* random-utility structure rather than replace it — which is exactly the framing the choice/Bayesian and structural chapters currently lack. A second cluster covers generative AI as research *instrument*: causal evaluation of unstructured treatments (JMR) and inductive theory building (JCP).

All DOIs below are Crossref-verified; author lists and volume/issue/pages were pulled from Crossref directly, not inferred. Papers still online-first carry no volume/issue yet, and their entries are marked accordingly.

## Recommended additions

### 1. Choice Models and Permutation Invariance: Deep Demand Estimation in Differentiated Products Markets
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Singh, Liu & Yoganarasimhan — *Marketing Science* — [10.1287/mksc.2024.0724](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2024.0724)
- **Why it matters:** Builds demand estimators from the *permutation-invariance* property that choice over an unordered product set implies, rather than bolting a neural net onto a logit. Gives a principled bridge between structural demand estimation and deep learning — a gap the book currently narrates as a tension rather than a solved design problem.
- **Home:** `50-choice-bayesian.qmd` (primary — as the modern counterpoint to the RUM derivation), `29-structural-models.qmd` (demand estimation), `59-choice-demand-seminar.qmd` (reading list)
- **What to write:** The section that motivates logit's functional-form assumptions should note that permutation invariance is the *structural* property worth preserving; flexibility can be bought without discarding it. Pairs directly with #3 below — cite them together, they are the same argument from two directions.
- **Replication:** none found — mark as unverified rather than linking a guess.

### 2. Evaluating Novel Unstructured Treatments with Generative AI: A Causal Prediction Framework
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Ellickson, Kar, Reeder & Zeng — *Journal of Marketing Research* — [10.1177/00222437261476639](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261476639)
- **Why it matters:** Treats an unstructured object (ad copy, image, message) as the treatment itself and uses generative models to predict counterfactual outcomes for *novel* variants never fielded. This is the missing link between the book's causal-inference part and its multimodal part, which currently barely reference each other.
- **Home:** `47-causal-inference.qmd` (primary), `49-text-as-data.qmd`, `58-multimodal-fusion.qmd`, `48-ai-ml-marketing.qmd`
- **What to write:** A short subsection on treatments that live in a high-dimensional unstructured space — where the estimand is not "effect of ad A vs. B" but a response surface over a generative latent space. Worth a worked R sketch in `47`, which is code-heavy.
- **Replication:** none found.

### 3. Representing Random Utility Choice Models with Neural Networks
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Aouad & Désir — *Management Science* 72(8), 6686–6701 — [10.1287/mnsc.2023.02189](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.02189)
- **Why it matters:** Shows which RUM classes a neural network can represent exactly, giving the approximation-theory backing for #1. Together they let the book say something precise instead of hand-waving about "ML for choice."
- **Home:** `50-choice-bayesian.qmd`, `32-preference-measurement.qmd`
- **What to write:** Cite alongside #1 — representability result first, estimator second.
- **Replication:** none found.

### 4. Generative grounded theory (GGT): Inductive theory building in the age of generative AI
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Schmitt, Hao, Pham & Hofstetter — *Journal of Consumer Psychology* — [10.1002/jcpy.70036](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcpy.70036)
- **Why it matters:** A named method for LLM-assisted inductive theory building, from senior CB authors. `62-theory-construction-seminar.qmd` is currently the only chapter mentioning grounded theory at all, and it predates this.
- **Home:** `33-qualitative-research.qmd` (primary), `62-theory-construction-seminar.qmd`, `48-ai-ml-marketing.qmd`
- **What to write:** Where the chapter covers coding and constant comparison, add what changes when the coder is generative — and the validity questions that follow (audit trail, researcher degrees of freedom, reproducibility of an LLM pass).
- **Replication:** none found.

### 5. Evaluating the Impact of Privacy Regulation on E-Commerce Firms: Evidence from Apple's App Tracking Transparency
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Aridor, Che, Hollenbeck, Kaiser & McCarthy — *Management Science* 72(8), 7009–7027 — [10.1287/mnsc.2024.06600](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06600)
- **Why it matters:** ATT is the single largest privacy shock in digital advertising and the book does not cite it — `22-privacy.qmd` currently discusses opt-out economics and Apple's nutrition labels (line ~435) but stops short of the firm-side consequences.
- **Home:** `22-privacy.qmd` (primary), `09-online-environments.qmd`, `10-advertising.qmd`
- **What to write:** Extends the existing opt-out revenue discussion (~line 256) from the impression side to the firm side — acquisition cost, measurement degradation, and who absorbs it.
- **Replication:** none found.

### 6. Defaults and Decisions: Choice Architecture and Consumer Opt-Out
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Donkor — *Management Science* (online first) — [10.1287/mnsc.2024.05272](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05272)
- **Why it matters:** Direct extension of the `@lin2025choice` opt-out-defaults thread already running in `22-privacy.qmd` (~line 277).
- **Home:** `16-nudges.qmd` (primary), `22-privacy.qmd`
- **What to write:** Cite next to `@lin2025choice` — same mechanism, different setting; a good place to distinguish default effects from preference.
- **Replication:** none found.

### 7. A Representative Sampling Method for Peer Encouragement Designs in Network Experiments
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Li, Yang & Liu — *Marketing Science* — [10.1287/mksc.2022.0409](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2022.0409)
- **Why it matters:** Peer encouragement designs are the standard tool for identifying peer effects under interference, and the book covers interference (`47-causal-inference.qmd`, Wk 14 of the seminar map) without covering how to *sample* for one.
- **Home:** `54-network-graph.qmd` (primary), `47-causal-inference.qmd`, `60-field-experiments-seminar.qmd`
- **What to write:** Slots into the existing interference / graph-cluster-design material as the design-stage counterpart to the estimation-stage tools already there.
- **Replication:** none found.

### 8. The limits of reputation in platform markets: An empirical analysis and field experiment
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Nosko & Tadelis — *Quantitative Marketing and Economics* 24(1) — [10.1007/s11129-026-09313-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11129-026-09313-1)
- **Why it matters:** Same authors already cited in the book (Blake, Nosko & Tadelis 2015 on paid search, in `47-causal-inference.qmd` line ~552 and the seminar map line ~1395). This is the reputation-systems counterpart and the book's platform chapter should carry it.
- **Home:** `51-platforms.qmd` (primary), `09-online-environments.qmd`
- **What to write:** Selection bias in who leaves reviews, and why observed ratings overstate reputation's informativeness — a useful corrective wherever the book treats ratings as a quality signal.
- **Replication:** none found.

### 9. Virtual influencer marketing: A meta-analytic review
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Barari, Eisend & Kulczynski — *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* — [10.1007/s11747-026-01186-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01186-w)
- **Why it matters:** `15-influencer-marketing.qmd` has no coverage of virtual influencers at all; a meta-analysis is the efficient way to add the whole literature at once with effect sizes.
- **Home:** `15-influencer-marketing.qmd` (primary), `14-celebrity-endorsement.qmd`
- **What to write:** A subsection on human vs. virtual endorsers with the meta-analytic moderators, which also gives the celebrity-endorsement chapter a natural forward reference.
- **Replication:** meta-analytic coding sheets sometimes posted as supplementary material — verify at integration time; do not assume.

### 10. Are Critics More Credible? When Positivity Is Expected, Negative Reviewers Are Perceived to Be Less Credible
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Kim & Goodman — *Journal of Consumer Research* — [10.1093/jcr/ucag025](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucag025)
- **Why it matters:** Inverts the usual negativity-bias result — when positivity is the norm, negative reviewers *lose* credibility. A clean teaching contrast against the WOM material.
- **Home:** `09-online-environments.qmd` (primary), `06-virality.qmd`
- **What to write:** Cite where the chapter asserts negative reviews carry more diagnostic weight, as the boundary condition on that claim.
- **Replication:** JCR requires data/code deposit — check the article's supplementary link at integration.

### 11. Market response, individual-level choice and diffusion models: from Frank Bass to artificial intelligence
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Hanssens, Jain, Kalyanaram, Lehmann, Leone & Reibstein — *Marketing Letters* 37(1) — [10.1007/s11002-026-09834-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11002-026-09834-5)
- **Why it matters:** A six-author retrospective tracing market-response modeling from Bass to AI — near-ideal framing material for the modeling chapters and the history chapter.
- **Home:** `25-modeling.qmd` (primary), `26-analytical-models.qmd`, `01b-history-of-marketing-thought.qmd`, `05-market-entry.qmd` (diffusion)
- **What to write:** Use as the arc citation where chapters open by situating a model class historically. Ties the week's neural-choice cluster (#1, #3) back to the Bass lineage.
- **Replication:** n/a (review).

### 12. A policy change at JAMS: The role of reproducibility in science
- **Authors / Journal / DOI:** Sarstedt, Noble & Noble — *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* — [10.1007/s11747-026-01190-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01190-0)
- **Why it matters:** An editorial statement of a *current* journal policy. The Research Craft chapters discuss reproducibility in general terms; this pins it to what a top journal now actually requires of authors.
- **Home:** `43-reporting.qmd` (primary), `42-review-process.qmd`, `41-scientific-writing.qmd`
- **What to write:** Concrete submission-time expectations — the practical end of the reproducibility discussion the book already runs across many chapters.
- **Replication:** n/a (editorial).
